# RQ5 — The Translation Question

*Does the demonstrated local speedup translate into (a) velocity on new features in mature production codebases and (b) production quality/stability? Strategic-core memo, PROTOCOL §1.*

## 1. The question and why it matters

AI demonstrably speeds up individual tasks under favourable conditions. The exec narrative assumes this compounds into faster delivery and unchanged (or better) production quality at the system level — this memo tests whether the evidence actually supports that leap, because it is the single load-bearing assumption behind velocity-based ROI claims.

## 2. The answer, up front

**Local task speedup is real. Its translation into system-level delivery is PARTIAL, and much smaller than individual-level numbers suggest. Confidence: medium.**

The translation chain, assembled quantitatively across four rungs:

1. **Individual/within-adopter output gain (best case, single firm):** he-2026-2x-mandate's causal decomposition gives **1.46–1.72×** within-developer throughput (rising to 1.99× by month 9 on tool), against a raw aggregate pre/post ratio of 2.09× that bundles calendar drift and hiring. heilman-2026-microsoft's independent Microsoft dose-response design (E1/E2, not E3) finds **+40.5%** more PRs at highest Copilot-usage weeks — the same order of magnitude, different tool era and design.
2. **Cross-company delivery trend (population average):** dx-reock-2026 (Tier D, near-zero methodology disclosure) finds PR throughput up only **+7.76%** against AI usage up 65% across ~400 companies; Faros (Tier D, COI-flagged) lands at +16.2% on its matched PR metric. **These percentages are direction-only evidence — both sources are vendor telemetry and per the DoD no ratio may rest on them alone.** The Tier A witness for dilution is internal to he-2026 itself: its raw aggregate 2.09× shrinks to 1.46–1.72× once calendar drift and composition are stripped out, and its authors describe the firm as an "upper envelope, not representative" — so population-average delivery gains being far smaller than within-adopter pilot numbers is well-grounded in direction, while the popular "~10× dilution" ratio is not a citable number.
3. **The wedge is explained by three converging mechanisms**, not by any single study being wrong:
   - *Dilution*: population averages mix adopters, light users, and non-adopters; he-2026's own effect is concentrated in accumulated *use*, not one-off adoption, and its authors call their firm an explicit "upper envelope, not a representative industry average."
   - *The absorption bottleneck* (tension 6, CONFIRMED direction / magnitude unresolved): he-2026 shows human review coverage falling 89%→68%, substantive review roughly halving, per-reviewer load doubling against 3.1× PR growth, and total cycle time *rising* 22% despite throughput doubling. Faros and DX converge directionally (review time up hundreds of percent; unreviewed merges +31.3% per Faros); Agoda and Zendesk corroborate the mechanism narratively with no numbers of their own.
   - *Brownfield attenuation* (tension 2, direction unanimous / magnitude contested): he-2026 itself finds +44% (significant) in post-2022 repos vs +12% (n.s.) in pre-2022 legacy repos, inside the same best-case firm; Stanford's quadrant independently (though not author-independently) puts brownfield/high-complexity work at 0–10%.
   - *Transient-gain decay*: cursor-velocity-quality-2025 (same CMU lab as he-2026) finds Cursor-adoption velocity in OSS repos peaks at month 1 and decays to statistically indistinguishable from zero by month 3–6; agents-vs-ides-2026's IDE-first cohort decays *through* zero to −35% commits/−61% LOC by month 6 — a reversal, not just a fade.

## 3. Part (a) — velocity on mature codebases

**Direction is robust; magnitude is not settled. Confidence: medium on direction, low on any specific number.** Every source that measures a greenfield/brownfield-style contrast points the same way — gains shrink as codebase age, complexity, or unfamiliarity rises — and no source shows the reverse. But the sharpest quantifications (Stanford's quadrant, he-2026's repo-age cohort) share an author (Denisov-Blanch) and are not cross-group triangulation. The two genuinely independent E3 brownfield datapoints disagree on floor: he-2026's own legacy-repo cut is **+12%, not significant** — a near-zero floor, not negative — while metr-2025-rct finds **−19% measured slowdown** on highly familiar, 1M+-LOC OSS repos. google-rct-2024, a real brownfield T4 task (pre-agentic), found a positive +21–26% effect (contested significance), showing brownfield does not reliably net to zero even without agentic tooling. "Brownfield" is operationalised at least four incompatible ways across this evidence base — no single discount percentage travels across sources.

## 4. Part (b) — production quality/stability

**Directionally present, magnitude and defect translation unmeasured. Confidence: medium.** Measured proxies conflict on non-comparable constructs: he-2026 finds flat-to-slightly-better revert rates (−0.059***, AI PRs revert *less*) in its single firm; Faros finds incidents/PR +242.7% and bugs/dev +54% across 22,000 developers (Tier D, unaddressed organisational-growth confound); cursor-velocity-quality-2025 finds static-analysis warnings +30.3% and complexity +41.6% persisting after velocity fades. Quality debt is substantiated on two genuinely independent Tier A legs (CMU's complexity/warnings growth; agent-maintainability-2026's downstream maintenance-cost collapse for agent-on-agent code, up to 13.1pp lower task-resolve rate) — but the popular churn/duplication proxies are the *weakest*-supported version of the claim, and no source translates any of this into a measured defect rate in the agentic era. DORA's own throughput/stability split adds a self-report data point only: throughput flipped positive 2024→2025 while the instability association persisted both years — a contested flip inside a redefined instrument, not resolved evidence either way.

## 5. The strongest counter-evidence, stated fairly

he-2026-2x-mandate is the best single piece of evidence that translation *can* work at scale: a real, causal (staggered DiD, developer fixed effects, placebo-tested), sustained **1.46–1.99×** within-adopter throughput effect in a genuine mature enterprise codebase, with **flat-to-improving** revert rates — no visible quality collapse. google-rct-2024 independently found a positive brownfield effect pre-agentically. The unanimous-direction sources for attenuation (Stanford, he-2026's own legacy split, cursor-velocity-quality-2025, agents-vs-ides-2026) substantially share authorship (one CMU/Stanford cluster), so "unanimous" overstates independence. And no source in this evidence base measures true feature-level business velocity — PRs, commits, and epics are all proxies for delivered value, not delivered value itself; DORA and Stanford both flag PR/commit counts as directly gameable and misleading in named case studies.

## 6. The verdict paragraph

Local task speedup is real and, in the best-run firms, causal and substantial (1.5–2× within-adopter), but it does not translate one-to-one into system-level delivery: the only cross-company delivery figures available (single-digit-to-low-double-digit percent — DX +7.76%, Faros +16.2%) are Tier D vendor telemetry and carry direction, not a citable ratio; the Tier A evidence for the same conclusion is he-2026's own raw-versus-causal gap (2.09× → 1.46–1.72×) and its authors' "upper envelope" self-framing. Dilution, brownfield attenuation, and a shifted bottleneck all eat into the individual-level number before it reaches an organisation's delivery metrics. The binding constraint has visibly moved from code generation to review and verification capacity — confirmed in direction across five organisations (one measured firm, two vendor telemetry sets, two practitioner narratives), unresolved in magnitude. A production-quality/stability cost is directionally present in complexity, static-warning, and downstream-maintenance measures, but its translation into an actual defect rate is unmeasured anywhere in the agentic era, and the one rigorous single-firm causal study (he-2026) shows no quality collapse at all. **Overall confidence: medium** — direction is well triangulated across independent designs and populations, but the CMU/Stanford authorship cluster, the single-firm scope of the strongest causal evidence, and the absence of any true business-velocity or defect-rate measurement mean no specific magnitude should be quoted with confidence.

## 7. What this means for us

Set exec expectations at "modest, real, org-level gains — not the 2–10× headline number," and attribute the gap explicitly to review/verification capacity, not to AI not working: the individual-level number is genuine but will not show up undiluted in delivery metrics unless review and absorption capacity are funded alongside the tooling. Translation is most likely to work where he-2026's own conditions hold — a codebase actively invested in as "clean" (Stanford's cleanliness moderator, R²≈0.40), sustained (not one-off) adoption given months to compound, and deliberate investment in review/verification capacity so generation gains aren't simply queued up as unreviewed risk. Do not claim a feature-velocity number to the business without first checking whether review throughput and defect/incident rates moved in the same window — on current evidence, nobody has actually measured that pairing.
