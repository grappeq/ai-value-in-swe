# RQ3 — Failure Map: Where AI Is Useless or Net-Negative

## 1. Headline answer

Failure is predictable, not random. The single strongest piece of evidence is Tier A: METR's RCT found experienced developers **19% slower** (measured; 95% CI 1.3–39.5%) using early-2025 agentic tooling on their own large, mature, highly familiar open-source codebases — while believing themselves 20% faster (`metr-2025-rct`). Crucially, this is not an isolated result. Its own factor analysis names the same predictors that recur, independently, across every other design in this evidence base: familiarity, size/complexity, implicit context, and reliability. Stanford's talk-reported quadrant (`stanford-denisov-blanch`), He et al.'s objective repo-age cohort split (`he-2026-2x-mandate`), and CMU's cursor/agent-adoption DiD trio (`cursor-velocity-quality-2025`, `agents-vs-ides-2026`) all find gains concentrating in small, new, well-scoped, low-entropy work and shrinking — never reversing to a brownfield-positive pattern at population scale — as context grows. The predictors replicate across RCT, quasi-experimental DiD, and controlled-manipulation designs; what does not yet replicate independently is a single precise magnitude for the "brownfield discount" (tension-2 verdict: contested on magnitude, not on direction).

## 2. The failure-predictor list

**(a) High developer familiarity with the codebase.** METR's own 21-factor analysis names this as one of five likely contributors to its −19% result: more familiarity, more slowdown (`metr-2025-rct`, Tier A, E3-boundary). Counter-intuitive but mechanistically clear — familiar developers have rich implicit context an AI cannot draw on, so AI substitutes for cheap parts of the task while adding overhead on the expensive parts. **Confidence: medium-high** — one rigorous source, but internally consistent with (c) below.

**(b) Large, complex, mature codebases.** METR's factor analysis names "large and complex repositories" directly; Stanford's talk-reported quadrant shows brownfield/high-complexity gains collapsing to 0–10% and a log-scale decay from 1,000 to 10M LOC (Tier B, talk-grade); he-2026-2x-mandate's objective pre-/post-2022 repo-age cohort split finds +44%*** in newer repos vs +12% (n.s.) in legacy repos inside the same firm (Tier A). **Confidence: medium** — direction robust across three designs, magnitude not (tension-2 verdict).

**(c) Implicit context AI cannot access.** METR names this explicitly as a slowdown factor; Zalando's postmortem-analysis pipeline shows the mirror-image failure — general models give "unacceptable analysis" of Zalando-internal technologies (e.g. Skipper) absent from public training data (`zalando-2025-postmortems`, Tier C). **Confidence: medium** — one Tier A mechanism finding plus one convergent Tier C case.

**(d) Low-popularity languages.** Stanford's talk: Cobol/Haskell/Elixir show minimal-to-negative gains ("AI can actually decrease productivity because it's so bad at coding in it"), covering an estimated 5–10% of global dev work (Tier B, transcript-confirmed). **Confidence: medium** — single-source, but a clean, mechanistically obvious effect (training-data scarcity).

**(e) Dirty/high-entropy codebases.** Stanford: cleanliness correlates with AI productivity gains at R²≈0.40, with an explicit reinforcing-decline mechanism (unchecked AI use degrades cleanliness, which further collapses AI gains) (Tier B, transcript-confirmed). **Confidence: medium** — one source, but the feedback-loop framing is distinctive and decision-relevant.

**(f) Prior AI-IDE exposure.** `agents-vs-ides-2026`'s IDE-First cohort shows agent adoption's velocity payoff decaying through zero into significantly negative territory (−35% commits, −61% LOC) by month 6, versus large, persistent gains for Agent-First repos (Tier A DiD, E3). **Flag the rival interpretation directly:** this is a moderator of *tool-exposure novelty*, not of codebase maturity — the authors' own "higher coordination/integration cost" explanation is interpretive, not measured, and a first-time-novelty effect (initial excitement/experimentation fading) is at least as plausible a mechanism as genuine diminishing returns from AI saturation. **Confidence: medium** for the pattern itself; **low** for any maturity-based causal story attached to it.

**(g) Task type within otherwise-similar work.** `task-stratified-agents-2026` finds a 29pp acceptance-rate gap between the best (chore, 84.0%) and worst (perf, 55.4%) AIDev task categories (Tier B, E3) — performance work is the consistent weak spot. `churn-151-repos-2025` finds churn effects concentrate almost entirely in generation tasks specifically, with no consistent pattern in optimisation/maintenance work (Tier A RDD, but E1/E2 — explicitly excludes Claude Code). **Confidence: medium** for the task-type gradient existing; **low** for it transferring cleanly to the agentic era, since the churn evidence is pre-agentic.

**(h) Verification-heavy work where review cannot absorb the output.** `he-2026-2x-mandate` (Tier A DiD, E3): substantive human review fell from ~39–47% to ~21% of PRs as throughput doubled against a reviewer pool growing only 1.5× against 3.1× PR volume growth — the bottleneck moved to review, not resolved. This is tension 6's confirmed-in-direction verdict (five independent orgs, no dissenting source, per LEDGER.md Phase 3). **Confidence: high** for the pattern (rare five-way convergence); **medium** for magnitude (mostly Tier C/D beyond he-2026).

## 3. Failure modes in operation

Zalando's two-year field pipeline shows a persistent **surface attribution error (~10%)**: the model anchors on a prominently-mentioned component ("S3 mentioned but not causal") and builds a plausible-sounding but wrong causal narrative — unresolved by negative prompting, present even in Claude Sonnet 4 (self-reported/impressionistic, not audited — no stated n or CI; `zalando-2025-postmortems`). The same source reports a **no-code agentic RCA attempt tried and explicitly abandoned** ("performance limitations, inaccuracies, and hallucinations") — a rare, named negative agentic result. `review-agents-reality-2026` independently corroborates a related mechanism at scale: **CRA-only PR review is 60.2% noise** (of closed CRA-only PRs falling in the lowest signal band) and merges 23pp below human-only review (45.20% vs 68.37%, χ²=83.03, p<0.001) — an empirical rebuttal of vendor claims (Qodo's "80% no human comments needed") that had not survived contact with measured data.

Three further incident leads are **queued but not yet extracted** and should be treated as unverified leads, not evidence: a Replit agent production-database deletion/fabrication postmortem, an AWS Kiro 13-hour Cost Explorer outage (secondary-sourced "user error" framing, contested), and a developer-agent misalignment taxonomy built from 20,574 sessions (arXiv 2605.29442). None can carry a claim in this memo.

## 4. The skill-formation cost as a failure dimension

`shen-2026-coding-skills` (Anthropic RCT, N=52, Tier A-candidate, **E2 chat only — not agentic**) found AI-assisted learners scored significantly worse on a post-task comprehension quiz (d=0.738, p=0.010) than unassisted learners, **with no significant time-to-completion benefit**. This is a genuine net-negative use case: chat-delegation-style AI use while learning an unfamiliar library (Trio) cost comprehension and bought no measured speed. The authors' own hypothesis — that agentic tooling would show the same or worse effect via deeper cognitive offloading — is untested, not evidenced; do not carry it forward as agentic-era fact. **Confidence: medium-high** for the E2 finding itself (clean RCT); **low** for any agentic-era extrapolation.

## 5. Nulls that bound the failure story

Several feared failures did **not** materialise, and bound how far the failure narrative should be pushed:
- **Duplication is a null across every controlled design**: `agents-vs-ides-2026` (AF +7.92% ns, IF −0.94% ns), `cursor-velocity-quality-2025` (+7.03% ns), and `agent-maintainability-2026`'s regression (duplication-adjacent metrics non-significant) — three independent designs disagree with GitClear's population-level +81%.
- **Churn rose only pre-agentically, and even then it's contested**: `churn-151-repos-2025`'s peer-reviewed RDD finds pooled churn *fell* (0.68→0.50 line-based, p<0.05), with ~80% of repos showing no discontinuity — though this is E1/E2 only.
- **`he-2026-2x-mandate`'s revert rate stayed flat-to-negative** for AI-authored PRs (−0.059 to −0.067***, p<0.001) even as review coverage collapsed — the feared "AI code breaks more" outcome did not appear on this (coarse) proxy.
- **`unreviewed-ai-prs-2026`'s matched baseline**: AI-authored PRs received human participation at rates statistically indistinguishable from human-authored PRs in the same repos (30.1% vs 30.8%) — the "84% unreviewed" headline dissolves once matched.

## 6. Claims list with confidence

1. Gains shrink, and can turn negative, as familiarity/size/complexity/implicit context rise — direction robust across RCT, DiD, and talk-grade designs; magnitude not settled. **High** on direction, **low** on magnitude. Sources: `metr-2025-rct`, `stanford-denisov-blanch`, `he-2026-2x-mandate` (note: latter two share an author — one voice for magnitude).
2. Prior AI-tool exposure predicts a *fading-to-negative* trajectory independent of codebase maturity — genuinely distinct mechanism from (1), confounded with novelty effects. **Medium.** Source: `agents-vs-ides-2026` (single independent DiD, no replication).
3. Performance/optimisation work is a consistent weak task-type across both merge-rate and churn measures. **Medium** — two independent designs (`task-stratified-agents-2026` Tier B E3; `churn-151-repos-2025` Tier A E1/E2), converging in direction but not era.
4. Review capacity, not code generation, is where agentic throughput gains actually fail to translate into delivered value. **High** — five-org convergence, the strongest-corroborated tension in the whole evidence base (`he-2026-2x-mandate` + Phase-3 tension-6 verdict).
5. Chat-based AI delegation during unfamiliar-library learning measurably degrades comprehension with no speed offset. **Medium-high** for E2; **not evidenced** for agentic tooling. Source: `shen-2026-coding-skills`.
6. Popular quality-debt proxies (duplication, and to a lesser extent churn) systematically overstate the failure story relative to controlled designs; the real downstream cost (agent-code buildability) is driven by behavioural contract drift, not structural metrics. **Medium.** Source: `agent-maintainability-2026` (first clean independent leg, McFadden R²=0.069 — the static metrics explain almost nothing).
7. LLM-based root-cause/incident analysis carries a persistent, hard-to-eliminate surface-attribution failure mode. **Low-medium** — single practitioner source, self-reported/impressionistic, not independently audited (`zalando-2025-postmortems`).

## What this means for us

Don't push agentic tooling on **performance-tuning work, deep refactors of legacy/high-entropy repos, or root-cause analysis of proprietary infrastructure** without added guardrails — these are the task types where the evidence converges on failure or, at best, unproven benefit. Treat **codebase cleanliness and modularity as an AI-readiness precondition**, not a nice-to-have: Stanford's cleanliness R²≈0.40 and the CMU trio's persistent complexity/warnings growth both point the same way — investing in hygiene *before* wide AI rollout is plausibly higher-leverage than investing in tooling itself. Before scaling agent-authored PR volume, **size review capacity deliberately** — the review bottleneck (tension 6) is the best-evidenced failure mode in this entire research programme, and "automated review absorbs the queue" is not the same as "the queue's risk is absorbed" (CRA-only review's 23pp merge-rate deficit and 60% noise rate say otherwise). Finally, **do not let chat-style AI use substitute for hands-on learning of new codebases or libraries** by less-experienced engineers — the one RCT that tested this directly found a real comprehension cost with no offsetting speed gain, and the authors' own view is that agentic delegation would very plausibly make this worse, not better.
