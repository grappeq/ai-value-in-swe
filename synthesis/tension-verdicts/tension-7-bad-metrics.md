# Tension 7 — Bad-metric traps

## 1. The tension as pre-registered

PROTOCOL §5.7: *"Suggestion acceptance rate, lines of code, and 'AI-generated %' are all gameable or meaning-free. Collect explicit critiques for the RQ6 anti-metrics list."*

## 2. The anti-metrics list, with evidence

| Metric | Why it fails | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **LOC / lines added** | Rewards verbosity and scope creep, not delivered value; rises even as effective output stays flat. | `metr-2025-rct`: AI-allowed issues show **+47% LOC per forecast-hour** (scope creep — classified by METR among the ten unclear/mixed factors, not the five likely slowdown contributors; the LOC-inflation observation stands regardless), while completion time is *worse* (+19%). `agents-vs-ides-2026`: Agent-First repos show **+76.6% lines added** alongside **+34.9% cognitive complexity, +17.7% static-analysis warnings** — throughput and quality debt rise together, so raw LOC cannot distinguish value from bloat. `bockeler-2026-qcon`: Böckeler explicitly discredits the "12¢/100 lines" metric live on stage — *"leaving aside that lines of code is of course not a good measure of value."* `space-dx-core4`: SPACE's core warning that activity counts (commits, PRs, lines) do not indicate value delivered. |
| **Commit / PR counts** | Throughput proxies that mask rework and flat real output; gameable by splitting work into more, smaller commits. | `stanford-denisov-blanch` (finding 13): 350-engineer team, four months pre/post AI adoption — **PRs +14%**, but "effective output" **did not increase meaningfully**, quality score fell ~9%, and **rework increased 2.5×**. Speaker's own words: *"had this company... simply measured PR counts, they would have thought, hey, we're doing great... It might be [negative ROI]."* `task-stratified-agents-2026`: merge/acceptance rate is explicitly an **acceptance proxy, not a quality signal** — "PR acceptance does not equal code quality... merged PRs may contain bugs," and 58/64 pairwise agent comparisons on this metric are statistically indistinguishable once corrected. `he-2026-2x-mandate`: raw PR throughput doubled (2.09×) while total cycle time rose 22% and substantive human review halved — the count improved exactly as the underlying process degraded. |
| **Suggestion acceptance rate** | Conflates "reviewer let it through" with "AI helped"; can rise even as developers are objectively slower. | `metr-2025-rct`: developers accept **<44%** of AI generations while being measured 19% *slower* overall — acceptance and value move in opposite directions in the same study. `task-stratified-agents-2026` echoes the construct-validity critique directly, citing prior work (He et al.) that acceptance says nothing about defects, rework, or maintainability. |
| **"AI-generated %" of code** | Definitionally gameable (depends entirely on undisclosed attribution heuristics) and unvalidated even in careful academic use. | `uber-2026-claude-cost`: Uber's headline **"~70% of committed code is AI-originated"** carries **no disclosed methodology** — not stated whether git-blame heuristic, IDE proxy, or self-report; already drifted across secondary retellings (conflated with an unrelated 84%/95% adoption figure). `aidev-dataset-2026`: the academic-standard version of the same metric (agent-authored-PR attribution) rests on an **unvalidated metadata heuristic** — "no precision, no recall, no false-positive-rate estimate... appears anywhere" across both foundational papers — with a documented, agent-specific self-disclosure bias (Codex/Claude Code attribution differs structurally from Copilot/Cursor/Devin's). If the rigorous version of this metric is unvalidated, the vendor version is worse. |
| **Token/usage volume as value proxy** | Usage and value decouple; heavy usage can *underperform* moderate usage. | `stanford-denisov-blanch` (finding 11): token spend vs. productivity gain shows only a loose correlation (**r≈0.20**) with a **"death valley" around 10M tokens/month** — high-usage teams underperformed teams using fewer tokens. Speaker's verdict: *"AI usage quality matters more than AI usage value."* `uber-2026-claude-cost`: the mechanism case study — Uber's internal **leaderboards ranking engineers by token usage** directly incentivised the budget blow-out ("tokenmaxxing culture"), with the company's own COO on record: *"it's very hard to draw a line"* between usage stats and shipped value. |
| **Self-reported productivity multipliers** | Uninformative about measured change; can be internally incoherent even without a ground truth to check against. | `metr-2025-rct`: developers self-reported ~20% faster while measured 19% *slower* (~39–43pp gap). `stanford-denisov-blanch` (finding 10): a 43-developer self-rating exercise found self-assessed productivity percentile is "almost as good as flipping a coin" against the measured score. `vella-blincoe-2026`: 84% self-report improvement at both survey waves, yet self-reported productivity and self-reported experience (cognitive load, flow) are **statistically decoupled** (p=0.313) — self-report is internally inconsistent even with no measured comparator at all. |
| **Single-metric anything** | The foundational warning underlying every row above. | `space-dx-core4`: SPACE's core, verbatim, tri-confirmed claim: *"productivity... cannot be measured by a single metric or dimension."* DX Core 4's own four-dimension design deliberately pairs opposing metrics (speed vs. quality) specifically so that gaming one shows up as a cost on another — an explicit Goodhart's-Law countermeasure. |

## 3. What survives

Constructs that did real explanatory work across this evidence base, with controls attached: **within-developer throughput with fixed effects and a comparison group** (`he-2026-2x-mandate`'s DiD design, not the raw 2.09× ratio); **review coverage/latency and reviewer load** (he-2026's 89%→68% human-review coverage, 2.0× per-reviewer load — the clearest quantified bottleneck-displacement signal found); **revert/merge rates paired with a quality instrument, never alone** (he-2026's revert-rate null is credible only because paired with cost/review data; `task-stratified-agents-2026` insists on the same pairing); **rework share measured against a maintainability score, not just counted** (Stanford's 350-person case, `agents-vs-ides-2026`'s static-analysis/complexity deltas); **comprehension/skill measures** (flagged as a live gap, not yet well populated in this batch). All of these still carry real limits — none is validated against downstream defects or incidents, and several (Uber's committed-code %, AIDev's attribution) share an unaudited labelling layer underneath supposedly independent studies.

## 4. Era + independence audit

Largely era-independent: SPACE (2021, pre-LLM) and the construct-validity critiques transfer cleanly per PROTOCOL §2's methodology carve-out. The concrete illustrations are mixed-era but converge regardless: `metr-2025-rct` is E2/E3-boundary, `stanford-denisov-blanch` mixed E1/E2→E3, `he-2026-2x-mandate`/`agents-vs-ides-2026`/`task-stratified-agents-2026` are clean E3, `uber-2026-claude-cost` and `bockeler-2026-qcon` are E3 practitioner colour. Independence: `he-2026-2x-mandate`, `agents-vs-ides-2026`, and `stanford-denisov-blanch` share Denisov-Blanch as author — count as one voice, not three. `agents-vs-ides-2026`, `task-stratified-agents-2026`, and the AIDev provenance record are authorially independent but share one unvalidated attribution heuristic (`aidev-dataset-2026`) — also one voice for any claim about agent-PR rates specifically, though independent for the metrics-critique point itself.

## 5. VERDICT

**RESOLVED — high confidence.** The pre-registered claim is confirmed with specific, sourced evidence against every named metric (LOC, PR/commit counts, acceptance rate, "AI-generated %") plus three more this batch surfaced (token volume, self-report multipliers, single-metric designs). No source in this batch defended any of these metrics as standalone impact evidence; several (`task-stratified-agents-2026`, `bockeler-2026-qcon`, `space-dx-core4`) explicitly argue against their own headline metric's sufficiency.

## 6. What would change the verdict

A rigorous study showing one of these metrics tracks an independently-measured value outcome (e.g. acceptance rate correlating with defect-adjusted delivered functionality, controlling for task type) would weaken, not overturn, the verdict — the pattern is broad enough that one counter-example would qualify rather than reverse it.
