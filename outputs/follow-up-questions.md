# Open Questions for Follow-Up Research

**July 2026 · written after reading the two final deliverables with fresh eyes · planning material, not findings**

## A. Tensions inside our own findings

1. **Can the −19% and the +1.5–1.7× both be true today?** Our two best rigorous results point opposite ways (metr-2025-rct vs he-2026-2x-mandate). Candidate explanations: tool generation (early-2025 vs late-2025), one-off tasks vs ~9 months of practice, familiar OSS code vs enterprise setting. Nobody has re-run the slowdown design with current agents. If the slowdown was a transient early-tooling artefact, expectation-setting softens. *Chase: METR-style RCT on 2026 agents; METR's hinted autonomous-agent work.*
2. **Heavy use predicts gains, but heavy use also underperforms.** He-2026's compounding-with-tenure vs Stanford's token "death valley" (~10M tokens/mo). Likely reconciliation is skill-of-use vs raw volume — unproven. Directly bears on spend caps: do they trim waste or decapitate power users? *Chase: studies distinguishing usage patterns (not volume) of productive heavy users.*
3. **Does quality debt ever cash out?** Complexity rises persistently; agents degrade on agent-written code; but no link to defects/incidents/org-level slowdown is measured anywhere. One internal analysis (cursor-velocity-quality GMM) hints debt predicts future velocity loss — if true, current throughput is partly borrowed. *Chase: first org publishing incident/defect rates pre/post agentic adoption; SlopCodeBench-style multi-step drift work; the unextracted Amazon outage material.*

## B. Gaps we knowingly left

4. **Skill formation (deferred sweep).** One pre-agent-era, single-session RCT is the entire measured base. Does the comprehension cost persist and compound in professional work? *Chase: the dedicated sweep already queued in the ledger; anything longitudinal.*
5. **Five task types with zero impact evidence** (T1 requirements, T2 architecture, T9 internal tooling, T10 docs, T11 ops). Hard to measure, or just unexamined? Weight the follow-up by which of the five we spend most on. *Chase: targeted per-task-type search.*
6. **Seniority: two contradicting datapoints while companies act anyway.** Entry-level hiring is contracting (labour-market leads unextracted) on beliefs the evidence cannot support. What does an evidence-based junior pipeline look like in an agentic org? *Chase: first independent multi-firm seniority split; AI-native onboarding case studies.*

## C. Questions about the strongest finding

7. **What made the 2×-mandate firm work?** Everything rests on one self-described "near-ideal" company. The enabling conditions (codebase health? tooling? review culture?) may be more valuable than the effect size. *Chase: second/third enterprise DiD studies; close re-read of he-2026's context sections.*
8. **Can the review bottleneck be fixed?** Displacement is proven; resolution is not — no measured case exists of an org restoring substantive review without surrendering throughput. *Chase: intervention-style Tier C case studies (grey-box review, spec-driven development, verification tooling), late 2026.*
9. **Is the J-curve real?** Asserted everywhere, demonstrated nowhere — no longitudinal dataset shows the dip and the recovery. *Chase: longitudinal adoption studies.*

## D. The highest-value next step

10. **Run our own pilot.** Questions 2, 3, and 9 become answerable from our own data within ~two quarters using the instruments in internal-measurement-proposal.md. Pair it with a one-day external refresh when DORA 2026 (September) and Stack Overflow 2026 (Q4) land — noting the protocol currently scopes this project as one-time research, so a refresh is a human decision.

*Everything here builds on the existing evidence base — the ledger's Queued table still holds ~25 triaged, unextracted leads that map onto these questions, so a follow-up phase could start warm rather than cold.*
