## A. Tensions inside our own findings

1. **Can the −19% and the +1.5–1.7× both be true today? — PARTIALLY ANSWERED (R3), unchanged (R4).** Yes, and we can say why: METR's late-2025 re-run erases the slowdown but both new confidence intervals cross zero and the design is abandoned; the transcript analysis models a 1.5–13× ceiling; he-2026's 1.46–1.72× sits inside the corridor; effect size is setting-dependent at pool level. R4 adds one more axis to the reconciliation — *unit of output*: the largest new study (500,000 open-source developers, Tier B, Microsoft data) finds sync agents raise commits ~150% and PRs ~86% but releases only ~20%, so "how much faster" depends on which stage of the pipeline you count. *Still missing: any statistically significant causal agentic-era estimate. Nothing to chase.*
2. **Heavy use predicts gains, but heavy use also underperforms — OPEN, one new angle (R4).** The first qualitative description of *usage pattern* (as opposed to volume) arrived: professionals keep control — 9 of 13 observed review every change in their own domain, chunk plans into ≤6 steps, and drop code review on unfamiliar tasks. No outcome attached. *Chase: your own pilot; any telemetry that classifies sessions by pattern rather than token volume.*
3. **Does quality debt ever cash out in defects/incidents? — OPEN, narrowed again (R4).** (a) *Catastrophic tail*: the 0.07% figure now rests on eleven episodes on the paper's weakest-validated axis — say "of the order of 0.1%, imprecise". (b) *Structural debt*: two new persistence rows (22.7% of AI-introduced static issues survive at HEAD, no human baseline; agent merges draw follow-up fixes at 1.62× the odds, low absolute incidence) and an intra-dataset contradiction on who does the follow-up (83% human maintenance commits vs 70% same-agent fix PRs — different units, one corpus). Committed AI configuration halves the complexity cost in one observational study — a discipline marker is as likely as a guardrail. (c) *Detectability — now two independent data sources (a vendor postmortem; a lab benchmark whose authors overlap the AIDev group)*: with the model held constant, routine harness releases produced +52–131% token spikes and a 7-point solve-rate drop that passed all 300+ of the project's CI tests; Anthropic's own evals missed two regressions. **Standard test suites do not see agent-quality drift.** Nobody publishes defect rates pre/post agentic adoption (Duolingo's "for the most part, no" is informal revert counting; Qodo's 89% is recalled prevalence). *Chase: nothing external left; only your own pilot (Q10) produces a denominator.*

## B. Gaps we knowingly left

4. **Skill-formation sweep — DISCHARGED; watch PARTIALLY MET (R4).** An independent group (UMD/NYU/CMU, NSF) ran the first agent-condition comprehension trial: 54 students, randomised; the agent arm finished faster and more accurately, measured lower on comprehension (d>0.8), showed no deficit on a 20-minute no-AI extension task, and preferred the agent anyway. Two randomised legs now — one on crowdworker developers (chat), one on students (agent) — both single-session, both finding the comprehension cost. *Watch: any longer-horizon study on working professionals.*
5. **Unmeasured task types — PARTIALLY ANSWERED; T2 moves (R4).** Architecture/design (T2) moves from "confirmed dry" to "usage and perception evidence only" — 291 ChatGPT design conversations plus 65 practitioners; architecture tasks need the longest exchanges (median 11 rounds); no outcome measured. Debugging (T8) gains a further non-agentic source (Google's single-pass test-failure diagnoser: 90% expert-judged accuracy, 63% user helpfulness, no time saved measured) — still zero measured agentic-era effect. Requirements (T1) and internal tooling (T9) remain confirmed dry. *Chase: weight by your own spend on those types.*
6. **Seniority — CLOSED, unchanged (R4).** Only pre-agentic, cluster-internal direction evidence appeared (Copilot-access RDD: lower-prominence developers shift effort more). The two agentic-era datapoints still disagree. *No chase.*

## C. Questions about the strongest findings

7. **What made the 2×-mandate firm work? — PARTIALLY ANSWERED; sixth candidate condition (R4).** To the five conditions (young codebase, uncapped consumption, mandate as catalyst, review routed around, narrow homogeneous task shape with a verification harness) add a sixth, one voice and observational: *committed AI configuration* — repositories with rules/context files in version control show half the complexity growth at the same velocity, but most committed the configuration after adopting and were 4× more active beforehand. A regulated-bank single case (one staff engineer plus three agent tools delivered a four-person initiative in half the planned time; participant-authored) reads as a catalogue of the same conditions. *Chase: a second enterprise DiD — still none exists.*
8. **Can the review bottleneck be fixed? — OPEN; "queue vs function" reinforced with an independent academic leg (R4).** Across 1.02 million reviewed pull requests in 207 projects (own-mined, not the usual dataset), AI-reviewer involvement cut time-to-decision by 2.5–4.5 days per KLOC in two of three adoption patterns (none in the third) while a process-quality proxy stayed flat or worsened. Agentic PRs wait 5.25× longer for a first reviewer and are reviewed 1.5× *slower* once picked up (vendor telemetry). Duolingo's auto-approval bot and Itaú's unchanged lead time are two more routing-around cases. Still no case anywhere of substantive review *restored* at sustained throughput. *Chase: unchanged.*
9. **Is the J-curve real? — OPEN; hole persists (R4).** One vendor CI series moved back toward baseline between September 2025 and March 2026 (main-branch success 70.8% → 76.7%) — but it carries no AI variable, so it is only the absence of a durable decline, not a recovery curve. Still asserted everywhere, demonstrated nowhere. R5 (12 Oct): durability of gains re-labelled CONTESTED — the nine-month rise in he-2026 cannot be separated from model vintage, which sharpens the 'rise' half of this question as well as the 'dip' half.
