## A. Tensions inside our own findings

1. **Can the −19% and the +1.5–1.7× both be true today? — PARTIALLY ANSWERED (R3).** Yes, and we can now say why. METR's own late-2025 re-run erases the slowdown: point estimates flip positive (−18% and −4% task time) but both confidence intervals cross zero, METR reads them as a *lower bound* (30–50% of tasks refused without AI), and it is abandoning the design because no-AI control groups are no longer viable. Their transcript analysis models a soft *ceiling* of 1.5–13×. he-2026's 1.46–1.72× sits inside that corridor, and a Tier A meta-analysis shows effect size is setting-dependent at pool level (lab g=0.73 vs enterprise 0.19 ns vs OSS 0.01 ns). The two famous numbers sit at different points of tool-generation × setting × horizon × selection — not in contradiction. *What's still missing: any statistically significant causal E3 estimate. Nothing to chase; the scarcity is confirmed as the literature's own (10,186 records screened, 2/16 productivity effects agentic).*
2. **Heavy use predicts gains, but heavy use also underperforms — OPEN.** Unchanged by R3 extractions; the capped-vs-uncapped natural experiments (Q11) remain the best future signal. *Chase: usage-pattern (not volume) studies; our own pilot.*
3. **Does quality debt ever cash out in defects/incidents? — OPEN, substantially narrowed (R3).** Three channels now characterised separately. (a) *Catastrophic tail*: real but thin — 0.07% hard-to-reverse cases in 16k validated misalignment episodes; destructive existence proofs documented (Replit; Grigorev — where the human *approved* the destroy: automation bias). Amazon's "incident trend" is document-based journalism against an on-record corporate denial, unresolved, gone quiet. (b) *Structural debt*: independent architecture DiD is null-to-artefact (smells flat, volume up, worst-case zero); redundancy up but single-repo; humans do 83% of maintenance on agent files either way. (c) *Detectability — the new blocker*: Anthropic itself twice failed to detect user-visible quality regressions via internal evals, seven months apart (one drop later measured at 3%); user complaints were the alarm both times. **Nobody publishes defect rates pre/post agentic adoption, and the instruments to notice small regressions don't exist even at the vendor.** *Chase: nothing external left; only our own pilot (Q10) produces a denominator.*

## B. Gaps we knowingly left

Still no longitudinal professional agentic-era study. *Watch: replications of Shen & Tamkin with agentic tools.*

T1 (requirements), T2 (architecture), T9 (internal tooling) remain confirmed dry. *Chase: weight any further effort by our own spend on the three dry types.*
6. **Seniority — CLOSED at "confirmed dry" for the agentic era (R3).** The hunt is exhausted: the only candidate third voice (Opsera) carries a headline number untraceable in its own report (direction-only, Tier D, era-indeterminate), and the much-cited maintenance-burden paper turns out to study the 2021 Copilot preview (E1). The two E3 datapoints still disagree (flat gradient vs C-shape). New mechanism worth keeping: the E1 burden-shift finding — seniors lose own output while gaining review load — suggests seniority effects may be absorption effects in disguise. *No chase. Wait for the literature; revisit if a third independent E3 split appears.*

## C. Questions about the strongest findings

7. **What made the 2×-mandate firm work? — PARTIALLY ANSWERED (R3, from our own record + convergent practitioner detail).** Five evidence-graded enabling conditions: young codebase composition (gains +44% in post-2022 repos vs +12% n.s. legacy); uncapped consumption (20× token growth tolerated); the mandate acting only as catalyst (residual effect ≈0 — the channel is adoption + accumulated use, compounding ~9 months); absorption "solved" by routing around substantive review (the enabling condition that is also the cost); and — from Salesforce, converging with R1's migration pattern — narrow agent scope, structurally homogeneous tasks, a verification harness, mandatory review. *Chase: a second enterprise DiD — still none exists.*
8. **Can the review bottleneck be fixed? — OPEN; "queue vs function" reinforced (R3).** New function-degradation signals all point one way: reviewer sentiment is blind to measured redundancy; 79% of merged agentic PRs show no visible human feedback; rejections are coordination-dominated (only ~36% demonstrably agent failure; a further third has no recorded reason); humans keep 83% of maintenance. (PR-side signals are one voice — AIDev.) Still no case anywhere of substantive review *restored* at sustained throughput. *Chase: any org showing restored substantive review; otherwise the pilot's review-absorption metric.*
9. **Is the J-curve real? — OPEN; hole persists.** Nothing in R3's 20 sources shows dip-then-recovery in any organisation's own longitudinal data. Still asserted everywhere, demonstrated nowhere. *Chase: longitudinal adoption datasets; our own pilot (Q10).*
