# ai-spend-landscape-2026 — per-engineer AI tooling spend: the 2026 evidence landscape

**Landscape record (multi-artefact, defect-rate-side-b precedent) · assembled 2026-07-06 from a three-channel comprehensive hunt (corporate disclosures / surveys & market data / practitioner ground truth), with orchestrator verification of the one cross-agent conflict · RQ4**

## Verification status

Each item verified to the level stated per-item below. One cross-agent conflict resolved by direct verification: the "Microsoft $500–2,000/engineer/month" figure does NOT trace to any Microsoft source (see item 4).

## The headline structural finding: a seat→token regime change during 2026

Late-2025 budgets were seat-licence shaped (list prices $10–40/user/month; DX survey shows most leaders at $101–500/developer/YEAR). By mid-2026 the market is consumption-shaped: Gartner reports a quarter of leaders at $200–500/developer/MONTH on tokens; GitHub itself moved Copilot to usage-based billing (June 2026); Anthropic's own cross-customer cost estimate silently doubled in April 2026. **Any per-engineer spend figure is close to meaningless without a date and a seat-vs-token label.** The apparent 20–60x contradiction between the DX and Gartner distributions is mostly this regime change, not a measurement dispute.

Tier D (analyst); survey n not visible in free coverage; primary PR 403s to fetch — figures taken from consistent secondary coverage, flagged.
2. **DX engineering-leader budget survey, 15 Oct 2025** (n=275): 2025 spend **38.4% at $101–500/developer/year; 10.5% at $501–1,000; 10.5% >$1,000/year**; ~50% of 2026 budget-holders allocating 1–3% of engineering budget; "$1,000/dev/year" cited as an emerging target. Tier D (DX vendor, COI); blended seat+consumption not separated; **seat-era baseline** — do not average with 2026 token-era figures.
3. **Anthropic Claude Code cost telemetry** (vendor docs, revised 15 Apr 2026): average **$13/developer/active day**; **$150–250/developer/month** across enterprise deployments; 90% of users below $30/active day. Tier D (vendor self-telemetry, COI — but the widest denominator available). **The estimate silently doubled** (~$6→$13/day) in April 2026, attributed by Anthropic to Opus 4.7 usage patterns with "no pricing change" — the doubling is itself an RQ4 datapoint (per-engineer consumption growing at constant prices).
4. **Microsoft E+D Claude Code pullback, 14 May 2026** (event VERIFIED: Tom Warren/The Verge scoop; corroborated by Forbes, Windows Central, The Next Web — licences cancelled for the Experiences+Devices division, Copilot CLI cutover by 30 Jun 2026, cost pressure cited by Warren's sources). **The circulating "$500–2,000/engineer/month" figure attached to this story is NOT a Microsoft disclosure**: full-text checks of Forbes and winbuzzer found no figure; Warren's scoop announcement carries none; secondary coverage uses the range explicitly as *Uber context*; one aggregator states outright it is "exactly what Uber saw". **Corruption instance #15: a real event wearing another company's number.** What the event DOES evidence (Tier C, press-sourced): a second major organisation taking cost-driven policy action on agentic-tool spend — the qualitative pattern (spend high enough to force policy) replicates; the number does not.

Tier C (anonymised-by-industry, same class as the 2x-mandate firm). ## What this can and cannot support

CAN (with grades): the two-regime structure (seat-era ~$100–1,000/dev/year → token-era ~$150–500/dev/month averages) [D+C triangulated, medium]; a real, policy-relevant heavy tail ($500–2,000+/dev/month for sustained agentic use — Uber's disclosure, Gartner's 6% bucket, PragEng's anecdotes, and two cost-driven policy actions) [medium]; consumption growing fast at constant prices (Anthropic's silent doubling; the 15x startup trajectory; he-2026's 20x) [medium]; caps/policy responses as the emerging control (Uber's cap; Microsoft's pullback; games-studio seat rejection) [medium-high].
CANNOT: any precise per-engineer average across the industry (no invoice-grade multi-org dataset exists — every number is vendor telemetry, press-sourced, executive self-report, or survey buckets); any cost-to-value ratio (the cost-without-outcome gap persists in every single item above).

## The scarcity finding, sharpened

Invoice-grade practitioner disclosure (own dashboards, published bills, named team sizes) is almost entirely absent from the public record. The "$150–250 average / $500–2,000 tail" consensus is a triangulation of weak-provenance sources that happen to agree — and at least one apparent corroboration (Microsoft's "figure") is actually the same source (Uber) laundered. Independence-checking spend claims is now as important as independence-checking productivity claims.
