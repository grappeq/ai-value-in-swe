## Tier & era & independence

- **Tier: C**, and arguably weak-C — graded down from the nominal "METR RCT-adjacent" pedigree for the same reason as metr-2026-uplift, but with additional erosion specific to this instrument:
  - **Entirely self-report, no measured/telemetry comparator of any kind** (confirmed on the general pass and not contradicted on either verbatim pass) — this alone caps it well below Tier A/B regardless of institutional quality, per PROTOCOL §3/§4 (measurement_basis must be recorded, and self-report-only sources cannot "carry a claim on its own" the way a telemetry- or RCT-anchored source could).
  - **~2% response rate** for the email-recruited channel (higher among METR staff/networks — a further selection skew toward METR-adjacent, AI-enthusiast respondents).
  - **Recruitment channels are convenience/self-selected**: GitHub, academic directories, METR's own networks, and X/Twitter — not a representative panel.
  - **Data-quality holes self-disclosed by METR**: 10 responses (~3%) filtered as anomalous; the occupation subgroup counts (87 + 71 + 129 + 48 = 335) do not sum to the stated total n=349, and the post does not explain the 14-respondent gap (I could not resolve this from the published text).
  - **METR itself flags scepticism about magnitude** — see Key findings §4 below — which is exactly the Tier-C-appropriate candour pattern seen in metr-2026-uplift, not a COI issue. Not Tier D: METR is a non-profit with no vendor stake in the answer, and ~70% of respondents were paid (avg. $200) with no result-contingent incentive disclosed.
  - Net: same institutional non-profit independence as the anchor and the uplift update, but the *weakest* method of the three METR sources graded so far (blog + self-report + low response rate + no data release, vs. the anchor's full RCT + released data, or the uplift update's at-least-randomised-but-troubled panel design).
- **Era: mixed, majority E3-leaning by respondent self-description, but not cleanly attributable.** Respondents average 12 years' programming experience, 19 months using AI for programming, 7 months using *agentic* AI tools specifically; "50% regularly use Claude Code" (Codex also mentioned as a reference tool, no adoption % given). This points to a majority-agentic-era respondent pool at survey time (Feb–Apr 2026), consistent with E3. However: (a) the survey does not break out its headline multipliers by era/tool-modality, so an E1/E2 minority is folded into the same medians without a way to separate their contribution; (b) a majority of the *sample itself* is out of this project's task scope — see Method & population below. Tag: **E3 (self-reported, tool-mix not isolable), with a scope caveat.**
- **Independence:** Independent non-profit, same institutional lineage as metr-2025-rct and metr-2026-uplift. No vendor sponsorship identified. Carries over the prior sources' minor flags (METR's commercial evaluation relationships with frontier labs; recruitment via METR's own professional networks — here amplified, since METR staff and their networks are an explicit, disproportionately-responding recruitment channel) and adds one new self-inflicted flag: the survey enumerates its own consistency and magnitude concerns candidly (§4 below), which raises credibility for the *caveats* but does not rehabilitate the headline numbers.

## Method & population

- **Design:** Cross-sectional self-report survey, fielded February–April 2026, asking about "March 2026" as the reference point, with paired retrospective (March 2025) and prospective (March 2027) estimates for the same value questions — a single-wave design with three time-points elicited *from memory/forecast*, not three actual waves of measurement.
- **Population:** n = 349 after filtering (10 anomalous responses removed from a larger raw pool; ~3% filter rate). Self-reported occupational breakdown: 87 software engineers, 71 researchers, 129 academics/PhD students, 48 founders/managers — these four figures sum to 335, a 14-respondent discrepancy from the stated n=349 that the post does not explain and that I could not resolve. **Only ~25% of the sample (87/349, using the stated subgroup count) are software engineers**; the remaining ~75% are researchers, academics/PhD students, and founders/managers — categories that fall outside or at the margin of this project's SDLC scope (PROTOCOL: "excludes adjacent knowledge work... unless a source covers it incidentally"). Demographics: ~48–50% US-based, ~25% Europe, ~37% academics/PhD students (as a share of respondents with that status, not a clean partition against the occupation categories above — the two demographic cuts are not obviously mutually exclusive in the source, which is itself a minor internal-consistency wrinkle).
- **Recruitment:** GitHub, academic institutional/conference directories, METR itself, METR staff's professional networks, and X (Twitter). Response rate ~2% for cold email recruitment, "significantly higher" for METR-affiliated channels (unquantified). ~70% of respondents received payment, average $200.
- **Questions asked:** one question on self-reported *speed* increase due to AI access (as of March 2026); three questions on self-reported *value* increase (conceptualised as holistic contribution, not just time), each with a March-2026 estimate plus retrospective (March 2025) and prospective (March 2027) companion estimates. Additional items: work type/dev environment, example AI tools used and instances of non-use, time allocation by activity, years of programming experience, LLM/agentic-tool experience, willingness-to-accept (WTA) to give up AI-tool access, and AI tool spend. **Exact question wording is not recoverable from the published post** — the methodology section describes the questions conceptually and the appendix reproduces them only as embedded images, not transcribed text.
- **No measured/telemetry/behavioural comparator of any kind is paired with these self-reports.** This is a perception-only instrument end to end; nothing in the post ties any respondent's stated multiplier to an independent record of their actual output, commits, or time.

## Key findings

All findings are **self-reported** (measurement_basis = self-reported throughout; there is no "measured" column to compare against in this source). Era tag: E3-leaning/mixed, unresolved by tool-modality breakdown (see above). Task-type tags: **not assignable per-task** — the survey asks about respondents' work holistically, not per SDLC task type, so no T1–T11 tag is defensible beyond a blanket "general technical work, ~25% of which is software engineering."

1. **Headline value multiplier (self-reported, March 2026 vs. no-AI counterfactual):** median self-reported **1.4–2x change in the value of their work** due to AI tools, across the three value-framing questions (the post itself frames this as a range across its three operationalisations, not three separate point estimates it names individually). [self-reported; whole-sample n=349]
2. **Headline speed multiplier (self-reported):** median self-reported **speed change of 3x** — explicitly flagged by METR as "expect[ed] to be higher than value change," i.e. METR itself treats the speed question as the more inflation-prone of the two framings. [self-reported; whole-sample n=349]
3. **Time-series self-report (retrospective + prospective, same value question, elicited in a single pass, not three actual waves):** median self-reported value **1.3x for March 2025** (retrospective), **~2x for March 2026** (current), **2.5x forecast for March 2027** (prospective). [self-reported; all three points are recalled/forecast from the same 2026 survey instant, not independently measured at each date]
4. **METR's own scepticism about magnitude — verbatim, itemised (self-critique of their own instrument):**
   - "There are tentative reasons to be skeptical of the magnitude of responses."
   - "survey results are not necessarily grounded in reality."
   - Direct callback to the anchor RCT: "our study in early 2025 found that people overestimated AI's effect on their time spent on tasks by 40 percentage points on average" — METR explicitly invokes its own perception–reality-gap finding (metr-2025-rct) as a reason to distrust this survey's magnitudes.
   - "Early qualitative investigation of public outputs plausibly suggests that especially high change in value estimates are overstated": of 7 responses reporting ≥10x value change, manual review judged 2 as overstating their actual change, 2 as "somewhat suspicious," with an overall impression that "respondents are giving larger answers than they would if they thought about the question for longer."
   - General framing note (paraphrased from the fetch, not a direct quote I can independently confirm word-for-word): public self-report estimates of AI's productivity effect have tended to run higher than estimates from field experiments or quasi-experiments — consistent with, and offered by METR as a generalisation of, the perception–reality gap already documented in the anchor.
   [All of the above: self-critique/methodology finding, directly on-point for RQ2/RQ6 — METR is using this post to argue against taking its own headline multipliers at face value.]
5. **Subgroup directional patterns (self-reported, no effect sizes given per subgroup):** higher perceived gains reported among respondents who use AI more frequently, who have more AI experience, and who use Claude Code or Codex specifically; **METR's own staff report lower perceived value gains than external respondents** — a notable null/negative-leaning internal contrast the post volunteers rather than suppresses. [self-reported; directional only, no quantified subgroup deltas published]
6. **Willingness-to-accept (self-reported):** median WTA to retain AI-tool access ≈ **29% of one month's salary**, with the post itself flagging "surprisingly high variance," including some respondents who would apparently pay over 100% of their estimated wage — treated by METR as a data-quality/noise signal, not a clean estimate. [self-reported; high dispersion explicitly noted]
7. **No measured/telemetry comparator anywhere in this source** — restated because it is the single most important scoping fact: every figure above is a perception, and this source cannot itself validate or falsify any of them against an outcome record.
8. **Tool/era context (self-reported):** respondents average 19 months' AI-for-programming experience and 7 months' agentic-tool experience; 50% regularly use Claude Code. No autocomplete-vs-chat-vs-agentic breakdown of the headline multipliers is given, so the era composition of who is answering "3x speed" cannot be isolated.
9. **Methodology recommendations METR draws from this exercise (not a finding):** favour "value" framings over "speed" framings in future instruments; survey managers/productivity researchers rather than individual contributors; shorten the survey; redesign the WTA question around actual temporary access removal rather than hypothetical valuation.

## What this can and cannot support

**Can support:** a documented instance of self-reported AI-value perception in early 2026 running very high (median 1.4–3x depending on framing) among a self-selected, AI-engaged respondent pool, alongside METR's own explicit, source-internal argument for distrusting that magnitude — useful as a fifth data point for the RQ2/RQ6 perception-instability literature and as a case study in survey self-critique. Useful as a *lead generator* (it names its own weaknesses) rather than as effect-size evidence.

**Cannot support:** any claim about actual (measured) 2026 productivity or value change for software engineers specifically, or for any occupation. Reasons stack: (a) no measured comparator exists in this source at all; (b) only ~25% of respondents are software engineers, and the headline multipliers are not broken out by occupation, so no SWE-specific figure can be extracted even at the self-report level; (c) METR's own qualitative review found some of the most extreme responses were probably overstated; (d) the 335-vs-349 sample-count discrepancy is unresolved. This source must never be cited in the evidence table with an effect size attributed to "developers" or to task types T1–T11 — at most it supports a general, occupation-mixed, self-report-only value/speed perception finding, tagged clearly as such.

## Cross-referencing (reported, not written to contradictions.md/evidence-table.tsv per task constraints)

**Tension 1 (perception–reality gap) — this is a fifth datapoint, and it sharpens rather than resolves the existing "direction is not consistent" entry.** The contradictions.md record currently reads: metr-2025-rct (over-estimate, experienced OSS devs, brownfield) vs. peng-2023-copilot-rct (under-estimate, freelancers, greenfield) vs. stanford-denisov-blanch (near-coin-flip miscalibration) vs. stray-2026-nav (near-zero correlation, ρ≈0.17, ns). This survey adds a data point that is directionally an *over-estimate* candidate (self-reported 3x speed / 1.4–2x value, against a backdrop where METR's own prior RCT showed ~40pp self-report inflation), but with three important qualifiers that keep it from cleanly extending the "over-estimate" side:
  - It has **no measured comparator of its own** — unlike metr-2025-rct and peng-2023-copilot-rct, which paired self-report against a measured outcome in the *same* study, this survey only *cites* the anchor's 40pp finding as a reason for scepticism; it does not independently re-measure anything. It cannot be triangulated the way the four existing datapoints were, because there is nothing on its "measured" side to compare its "self-reported" side against.
  - Its population is majority non-software-engineer (researchers, academics, founders), diluting comparability with the anchor's experienced-OSS-developer population even at the self-report level.
  - The magnitude (median 3x speed) is far larger than the anchor's post-hoc perceived 20% speedup, which — if anything — suggests self-report inflation may be *growing* over the 2025→2026 window (or that this is a more AI-enthusiastic/self-selected sample, or that "value/speed multiplier" framing elicits larger numbers than "% time saved" framing; the source cannot distinguish these). This is a framing/population confound, not evidence that developers' calibration has changed.

  **My read for cross-ref purposes:** this source should be logged as reinforcing the existing contradictions.md conclusion — "self-report magnitude is uninformative about measured change" — by adding an instance where the *source itself* pre-emptively distrusts its own magnitude, rather than by adding a clean fifth point on the over/under-estimate axis. I recommend it be filed as a "self-report growing more extreme, no comparator" addendum if/when contradictions.md is next updated, not averaged into the existing four-datapoint reconciliation.

**metr-2026-uplift's "belief that speedup improved" claim — this survey lends self-reported colour to that belief but does not validate it as a measured fact.** The Feb 2026 uplift-update post's claim that developers are "likely more sped up... in early 2026" was explicitly anecdotal (sourced to conversations with study participants, "very weak evidence," no effect size attached — per metr-2026-uplift's record, this must never be cited alongside a percentage). This May 2026 survey is the first source to attach a number to a similar-flavoured belief (median ~2x value / 3x speed for March 2026), and a naive reading would treat it as "the number behind the belief." That reading should be resisted: this survey (a) is self-report only, (b) is majority non-SWE, (c) is explicitly flagged by METR itself as probably overstated at the tails, and (d) was not designed as a follow-up to the uplift-update's developer panel — it is a separate, broader, differently-recruited instrument. **Net: this source neither confirms nor supplies the missing effect size for the uplift-update's belief statement.** It is best read as an independent (weak) signal that AI-enthusiasm/self-reported-value perception is high and rising among AI-engaged technical workers in early 2026 — consistent with, but not evidence for, the specific claim that measured developer speedup has improved since the −19% anchor finding.
