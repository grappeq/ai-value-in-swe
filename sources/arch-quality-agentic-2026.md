## Dataset provenance (explicitly checked, per task instructions)

**Own collection, not a shared public agentic-PR dataset.** The 151-repository monthly panel (74 treatment / 77 control, 1,811 Arcan snapshots) was mined and curated by the authors from scratch via a four-phase pipeline: GitHub mining (204 treatment candidates from artifact-based detection + 580 control pool) → propensity-score matching (90+90 pairs) → monthly snapshot extraction (175 repos, 2,241 snapshots) → Arcan static analysis (1,811 successful snapshots, 151 repos after attrition). This is **not** AIDev or any other shared/public agentic-PR corpus — no shared-provenance independence flag applies. The paper does, however, explicitly position itself as replicating/extending He et al.'s (2025/2026, MSR) design "one abstraction level higher" (SonarQube code-level → Arcan architecture-level), using a *different*, independently-mined repository set (151 vs. He et al.'s 806). Worth noting for cross-referencing: He et al.'s companion paper on Cursor-adopting repos (code-level warnings +30%, complexity +41%) is cited repeatedly throughout as the paper this one is in dialogue with — if `sources/cursor-velocity-quality-2025.md` already covers He et al., these two sources should be cross-referenced explicitly in synthesis as they discuss the *same underlying phenomenon at two different abstraction levels* using non-overlapping data.

## Grading

**Tier: B** (structured observational — large-scale telemetry/repo-mining with counterfactuals, but non-randomised, self-selected treatment). One-line justification: adoption of agentic AI tooling is observational and self-selected (repositories chose to adopt; treatment is not assigned), so despite a highly rigorous quasi-experimental identification strategy (staggered DiD with the Borusyak imputation estimator, propensity-matched controls, parallel-trends falsification via event study, Bacon decomposition, wild-cluster-bootstrap and Lee-bounds robustness), this cannot claim RCT/controlled-field-experiment status — it sits at the top of Tier B, materially more rigorous than a typical multi-company code-quality report, and the authors themselves are unusually disciplined about stating where the design still leaves room for doubt (see attrition caveat below).

**Era: E3 (agentic).** Confirmed by modality, not year: treatment is defined specifically as *observable agentic* usage (tool-specific config files — `.cursorrules`, `CLAUDE.md`, `AGENTS.md`, `.aider.*` — plus `Co-Authored-By` commit trailers), explicitly excluding inline-autocomplete-only usage, which produces neither artifact. Tools covered: Claude Code (n=44 in final sample), GitHub Copilot (n=14, agentic mode), Cursor (n=15), Codex (n=1), one repo also using Aider.

**Independence/COI:** Academic authors (SDU Denmark), no vendor funding disclosed, no Tier D flag needed. Independent data collection (see provenance above). No conflicts identified.

**Population:** 151 mature open-source Java repositories (74 treatment, 77 propensity-matched control). Comparable baseline size (mean 44.9 vs. 45.2 KLOC), maturity (73.8 vs. 80.1 months), popularity (918 vs. 920 stars). Repo-level unit of analysis — **no developer-seniority data**, so this source cannot speak to the seniority-asymmetry tension (§5.5). Scope is established/mature OSS Java systems only; explicitly does **not** generalise to greenfield projects (where the authors flag AI could influence architecture "from inception") or to enterprise/closed-source codebases.

## Reading notes (full text)

The paper tests whether code-level degradation found by AI-adoption studies (He et al. 2025/2026: +30% static-analysis warnings, +41% complexity in 806 Cursor-adopting repos) propagates to the *architectural* level — i.e., whether local code-quality erosion shows up as structural anti-patterns (cyclic dependencies, hub-like components, unstable dependencies, god components) in the dependency graph. This is exactly the kind of "does local degradation aggregate to system-level harm" question that matters for RQ3/RQ5.

**Headline result and why it's misleading at face value:** architectural smell density (ASD = smells/KLOC) declines 6.7% (p=0.004) in AI-adopting repos relative to matched controls. Read naively this says "AI improves architecture." The authors' own decomposition (RQ4) shows this is false: raw smell counts are statistically flat (+1.1%, p=0.82 — a clean null) while lines of code grow substantially faster in adopters (+12.8%, p=0.003). The density metric falls purely because the denominator (codebase size) grows while the numerator (smell count) does not follow proportionally. The paper's most transferable contribution is arguably methodological, not empirical: **any density-normalised metric (defects/KLOC, vulnerabilities/KLOC, churn/KLOC) is at risk of exactly this artefact whenever treatment plausibly affects codebase size**, and this is directly relevant to how we interpret any other density/rate metric appearing elsewhere in this evidence base.

**Per-type breakdown (RQ2):** of four smell types, only hub-like dependency density survives Holm multiple-testing correction (-5.0%, Holm-adjusted p=0.003). Cyclic dependency shows a similar point estimate (-5.0%) but fails correction (Holm-adjusted p=0.070). Unstable-dependency *raw counts* (not density) show a suggestive *increase* (+5.8%, raw p=0.032, Holm-adjusted p=0.128) — the one thread in the data pointing toward degradation rather than neutrality, but it does not survive correction and the authors are explicit that it is "a hypothesis for replication, not a finding." God-component effects are null but explicitly flagged as underpowered (77.5% zero-inflation in the outcome).

**Trajectory / persistence (RQ3) — the most decision-relevant result for us:** the event study shows the ASD effect is *not* static and does *not* decay. It starts at -5.5% in the first post-adoption month and grows monotonically to -9.5% by month 6, with no inflection visible by the end of the observed window. Pre-trends are flat (Wald χ²=1.64, df=5, p=0.896), supporting parallel trends. The authors explicitly caution that six months is a "preliminary" window and that longer observation is needed to see whether smell counts eventually catch up with LOC growth (which would close the density gap) — i.e., **we do not know from this study whether the pattern is stable long-term or a leading indicator of later architectural catch-up/degradation.**

**Robustness is unusually thorough for a Tier B source:** wild cluster bootstrap (-6.0%, p=0.010), Bacon decomposition (89.1% of DiD weight from "clean" treated-vs-never-treated/not-yet-treated comparisons), placebo test at a fake adoption date (null, p=0.427), tool-stratified estimates (all three major tools individually negative and significant: Claude Code -5.1% p=0.039 n=44, Copilot -4.0% p=0.005 n=14, Cursor -13.5% p<0.001 n=15, though dose-response interaction across tools is non-significant, p=0.716, so tool differences are exploratory only), and a control-contamination correction (8.9% of matched controls showed AI markers during the study window; correcting for this attenuation *strengthens* the estimate to -7.4%, not weakens it).

**The one real crack in the result — differential attrition:** 16/90 treatment repos (17.8%) vs. 13/90 control repos (14.4%) were lost during the pipeline, concentrated among the *largest* projects (Arcan/JVM memory failures). Under Lee (2009) worst-case attrition bounds, the effect bracket is [-9.9%, -2.3%] — both bounds negative, **but the upper bound's 95% CI includes zero (p=0.239)**. The authors state plainly: "under worst-case attrition, the effect cannot be distinguished from null." This is the load-bearing caveat for the whole paper and should travel with the headline number anywhere it's cited.

**Scope/external validity, stated by authors:** mature Java OSS only; the effect may differ in greenfield projects (T3, the "known sweet spot") or under strict AI-governance regimes; construct is "observable agentic adoption" via artifacts, a purposive/conservative proxy that likely biases toward *under*-detecting true adoption (attenuation, not inflation).

## Extraction records (one per finding)

Each record below states: what was measured, exact effect size, measurement basis, and a one-line can/cannot-support verdict.

1. **Primary ASD effect (RQ1).** β=-0.0698 (SE=0.0239), 95% CI [-0.117,-0.023], p=0.004 → ASD -6.7% (Borusyak); Sun–Abraham concordance -6.6% (p=0.005). Measured (Arcan static analysis on git snapshots), not self-reported. **Can support:** a claim that observable density-normalised architectural-smell metrics decline under agentic AI adoption in mature Java OSS over a 6-month window. **Cannot support:** any claim that AI improves architecture — the authors' own decomposition rules this out.

2. **Raw smell count (numerator), null.** β=0.0108 (SE=0.0486), 95% CI [-0.085, 0.106], p=0.82 → +1.1%, statistically flat. Measured. **Can support:** absolute architectural-violation counts do not measurably change under agentic AI adoption in this population — a genuine null, reported with full diligence. **Cannot support:** any claim of quality improvement or degradation in absolute terms; this is a precision-limited null, not proof of exact equivalence.

3. **LOC growth (denominator).** β=0.1201 (SE=0.0401), 95% CI [0.042,0.199], p=0.003 → +12.8%. Measured. **Can support:** agentic-AI-adopting repos grow code volume faster than matched controls (a velocity/output signal). **Cannot support:** a quality claim on its own — it's the mechanical driver of finding (1), not a quality result.

4. **Per-type: hub-like dependency density.** -5.0%, p=0.001, Holm-adjusted p=0.003 (survives multiplicity correction). Measured. **Can support:** the one smell type with a robust, corrected-significant decline. **Cannot support:** generalisation to other smell types — three of four types do not survive correction.

5. **Per-type: cyclic dependency density.** -5.0%, raw p=0.023, Holm-adjusted p=0.070 (fails correction). Measured. **Can support:** nothing on its own; record as a non-significant trend after correcting for multiple comparisons.

6. **Per-type: unstable-dependency raw count (the one degradation-direction signal).** +5.8%, raw p=0.032, Holm-adjusted p=0.128 (fails correction). Measured. **Can support:** a weak, unreplicated hypothesis that AI tools may add imports to less-stable packages — explicitly flagged by the authors as "not a finding." **Cannot support:** any exec-brief claim; too weak and uncorrected.

7. **Per-type: god-component, null but underpowered.** Density p=0.877; 77.5% zero-inflation in the outcome. Measured. **Can support:** nothing — record as an underpowered null, not evidence of absence.

8. **Trajectory/persistence (RQ3) — effect grows over the observed window, does not decay.** Event-study coefficients: h=1 → -5.5%; h=6 → -9.5% (monotonic, still expanding at window end). Pre-trend Wald test χ²=1.64, df=5, p=0.896 (flat, consistent with parallel trends). Measured. **Can support:** within a 6-month post-adoption window, the density-decline effect is not transient — it strengthens over time rather than fading. **Cannot support:** any claim about what happens beyond 6 months; authors explicitly flag this as unresolved and call for 12+ month replication.

9. **Tool-stratified heterogeneity (exploratory, post-hoc).** Claude Code -5.1% (p=0.039, n=44); Copilot -4.0% (p=0.005, n=14); Cursor -13.5% (p<0.001, n=15); dose-response interaction across tools p=0.716 (non-significant). Measured. **Can support:** directional consistency across all three major agentic tools (same sign, all significant individually). **Cannot support:** any claim that one tool is architecturally "better" or "worse" than another — authors explicitly label this a replication hypothesis, not a finding, given small per-tool n and non-significant interaction test.

10. **Worst-case attrition bound (the critical caveat).** Lee (2009) bounds: [-9.9%, -2.3%]; upper-bound 95% CI includes zero (p=0.239). Differential attrition: 17.8% of treatment repos lost vs. 14.4% of controls, concentrated among the largest projects (JVM memory failures during Arcan analysis). Measured/methodological. **Can support:** a caveat that must travel with finding (1) — under the most pessimistic plausible attrition pattern, the result is statistically indistinguishable from a null. **Cannot support:** a confident, uncaveated headline claim; this is precisely the kind of caveat CLAUDE.md requires to travel with the effect size.

## Red flags / things to watch

- **Do not let the "-6.7% ASD" number travel without its decomposition.** In isolation it reads as "AI improves architecture," which is the opposite of what the paper actually concludes. If this number shows up anywhere else in the evidence base or synthesis without the +1.1%/+12.8% decomposition attached, that is a mis-citation risk.
- **Worst-case attrition CI includes zero.** This source cannot single-handedly carry a "no architectural harm" claim at high confidence; medium confidence at most, given the authors' own caveat.
- **Repo-level, not developer-level.** No seniority split possible; do not use this source for the seniority-asymmetry tension (§5.5).
- **Naming collision found in `sources/`:** `churn-151-repos-2025.md` already exists and shares the "151 repos" framing. Per blind-extraction rules I did not open it, but the orchestrator should check it isn't the same study double-logged under a different source_id, or a genuinely distinct study that happens to share a sample size (possible coincidence, but worth a two-minute check before evidence-table merge).
- **Likely overlap with `cursor-velocity-quality-2025.md`:** this paper discusses He et al.'s Cursor-adopting-repo study (code-level: +30% warnings, +41% complexity) extensively as its predecessor/foil. If that source is already logged, cross-reference explicitly in synthesis — the two studies (code-level vs. architecture-level, on different repo samples) together are more informative than either alone, and the paper's own reconciliation hypothesis (AI writes more complex code *within* modules while respecting *between*-module boundaries, citing the 33.8% non-co-occurrence rate between static-analysis warnings and architectural smells from Esposito et al. 2025) is worth carrying into RQ3 synthesis as an explicit, testable, currently-untested mechanism.
- **Scope is narrow:** Java-only (an Arcan tooling constraint), mature/established OSS only, 6-month post-adoption window only. Do not extrapolate to greenfield (T3), other languages, or long-run trajectories.

## Surprises

- The direction of the topline result is the opposite of what most readers would expect from the "AI degrades code quality" prior established by code-level studies (He et al.) — and the paper's real contribution is showing *why* that intuition doesn't mechanically transfer to architecture, not confirming or debunking it wholesale.
- The methodological point (density-normalised metrics conflate numerator/denominator effects whenever treatment affects system size) generalises well beyond this paper and is relevant to how we should read *any* churn/KLOC, defects/KLOC, or similar rate metric elsewhere in this evidence base, including from other AI-adoption sources already logged.
- The effect trajectory is still *expanding* at the end of the study window (month 6), not decaying — this is a genuine open question the paper flags rather than resolves, and cuts against treating this as a "settled, stable" result either direction.
