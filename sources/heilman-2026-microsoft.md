## Tier, era, independence

- **Tier: A** (by design). This clears the PROTOCOL §3 Tier A bar ("large-N telemetry with counterfactuals"): within-engineer two-way fixed-effects (engineer FE + week FE) PPML regression on 413,732 engineer-weeks, with an explicit conditional-independence assumption stated up front, plus a seven-test robustness/falsification battery including **two genuine placebo tests** (placebo treatment: non-coding M365 Copilot usage; placebo outcome: teammates' PRs) — a design feature this research programme has flagged as unusually rare and valuable (cf. he-2026-2x-mandate's own placebo test for the model-release confound).
  - **Important design contrast with he-2026-2x-mandate, worth carrying into synthesis:** this paper has **no adoption-event structure**. The sample is restricted to engineers with *nonzero* GHCP usage across the full 43-week window (an explicit sample-restriction rule, §2.5) — there is no never-adopter control group of the kind he-2026-2x-mandate's 113 never-adopters provide. The identifying variation is purely week-to-week *intensity* within an already-adopted population, not adoption timing. The authors are candid about this: they place themselves on "rung 1" (association) of Pearl's Ladder of Causation, with design choices narrowing the gap toward "rung 2" (intervention), and state explicitly: *"only a randomized experiment could close it definitively."* This is a materially weaker identification claim than he-2026-2x-mandate's staggered-DiD-with-comparison-group design, even though both clear the same Tier A bar on the protocol's stated criteria.
  - The estimand is explicitly an **efficiency effect** (a controlled direct effect in the causal-mediation sense, citing Acharya, Blackwell & Sen 2016): coding time and browser time are entered as controls, which *deliberately* absorbs any time-savings channel through which Copilot might let an engineer do the same work faster. The authors flag this as making their estimate conservative/a lower bound on the *total* productivity effect, not a full accounting of it.
- **Era: E1/E2 — not confirmed E3.** This is the single most important correction to carry forward. The paper aggregates telemetry across "GitHub Copilot's IDE integrations" — operationalised as weekly counts of "suggestions, prompts, and accepts" (§2.5, §3). This vocabulary spans classic inline-completion acceptance (E1) and chat-style "prompts" (E2). The **GitHub Copilot CLI is explicitly excluded** ("not generally available for most of the study window"), and there is **no mention anywhere in the paper of agent mode, Copilot Workspace, autonomous PR-opening agents, or any multi-file plan–execute–verify loop** — the hallmarks of E3 per PROTOCOL §2. Both capabilities existed within Microsoft's own product during the Feb–Dec 2025 study window, so some undifferentiated agentic usage may be commingled inside the aggregate "IDE integrations" telemetry, but the paper gives no way to decode the E1/E2/E3 mix, and its own vocabulary ("suggestions... accepts") reads as autocomplete/chat-telemetry categories, not agent-session categories. **Era assignment: E1/E2 (predominant), agentic content neither confirmed nor ruled out and not separately measured.**
  - This matters directly for how he-2026-2x-mandate cites this paper. He et al. call it "the within-engineer study closest to ours" — true of the *design* (within-person dose-response), but their own study is confirmed E3 (Cursor, Claude Code, custom agents) while this one is not. The "closest comparable design" framing should not be read as "closest comparable era."
- **Independence — strong COI, undisclosed.** All three authors are Microsoft employees (Engineering Thrive and CHISE are internal Microsoft engineering-productivity/research groups), studying Microsoft's own commercial product (GitHub Copilot, a Microsoft/GitHub property) using Microsoft's own internal engineers and Microsoft's own internal interactivity telemetry. This is a materially stronger COI than google-rct-2024 (Google-on-Google) or cui-2024-copilot-field (2 of 6 authors Microsoft-affiliated): here it is **3 of 3 authors, 100% Microsoft**, studying a Microsoft product on a Microsoft population.
  - **No funding or conflict-of-interest disclosure statement was found anywhere in the paper** — no acknowledgments section, no COI statement, no data-access statement. This absence is itself worth flagging: contrast with he-2026-2x-mandate, which explicitly states its studied company "did not participate in the analysis or writing" and that no company employee is an author. Heilman et al. make no equivalent statement because they cannot — the authors *are* the vendor.
  - This is a case where methodological rigour (Tier A by design) and independence (Tier D-grade COI) point in opposite directions; per protocol, tier is graded on design, and the COI is recorded as a flag that must travel with any claim drawn from this source.

## Method & population

- **Setting:** 16,223 individual-contributor Software Engineers within Microsoft's Cloud+AI organisation (explicitly restricted to ICs — no managers). 43 weeks, **23 February – 21 December 2025**.
- **Estimation sample:** 413,732 engineer-weeks, after two filters: (1) engineer-level — exclude anyone with zero completed PRs or zero GHCP-usage days across the full 43 weeks (removes engineers using an alternative assistant or a pure-OSS workflow untracked by the telemetry); (2) week-level — exclude engineer-weeks with <10 hours total computer activity (treated as leave/vacation; working weeks with zero *coding* hours are retained and enter with coding hours = 0).
- **Outcome:** PRs created (attributed to the week of creation, not merge), with a 28-day completion window; still-open PRs beyond 28 days excluded.
- **Treatment (dose) — "interaction depth":** total weekly count of GHCP suggestions, prompts, and accepts, split into non-zero terciles: **Zero (0 interactions, 32.5% of engineer-weeks), Low (1–41, 22.6%), Moderate (42–164, 22.5%), High (165–13,193, 22.4%)**. A secondary "breadth" measure (days/week with ≥1 GHCP interaction: None/Low(1d)/Moderate(2–3d)/High(4d+)) is used only as a robustness check (§4.7), not the headline treatment.
- **Model:** Poisson Pseudo-Maximum Likelihood (PPML, `fepois`) with two-way fixed effects:
  `ln E[PRs_it] = β1·CodingTime_it + β2·BrowserTime_it + β3·Low_it + β4·Mod_it + β5·High_it + δ_t + α_i`
  Engineer FE (α_i) absorb all time-invariant between-engineer differences (skill, role, team, task allocation); week FE (δ_t) absorb org-wide shocks (deadlines, holidays, reorgs); coding time and browser time enter in **levels** (not logs, so zero-coding weeks participate) after the authors demonstrate empirically (Figure 1) that a simpler fixed-rate offset model's proportionality assumption is false in their data. Standard errors are clustered at the **manager** level (deliberately more conservative than engineer-level clustering).
- **Stated identifying assumption (their words):** *"Conditional on engineer fixed effects, week fixed effects, coding time, and browser time, an engineer's GHCP usage intensity is not systematically driven by the same unobserved factors that independently determine their PR output that week."* Explicitly labelled untestable directly; the seven-test battery (below) probes its plausibility rather than proving it.

## Key findings (all **measured**, telemetry-based; none are self-report)

1. **Headline dose-response (Table 1).** Engineers complete **40.5% more PRs** in their highest-GHCP-usage weeks than in zero-usage weeks, holding coding and browser time constant.
   | Level | β (SE) | % change (SE, pp) | 95% CI (from Fig. 3) |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | Low GHCP | 0.190 (0.009) | +21.0% (1.1) | [+18.9%, +23.1%] |
   | Moderate GHCP | 0.332 (0.009) | +39.4% (1.3) | [+36.9%, +42.0%] |
   | High GHCP | 0.340 (0.011) | +40.5% (1.6) | [+37.4%, +43.5%] |
   | Coding time (per hour) | 0.021 (0.001) | +2.2% (0.1) | — |
   | Browser time (per hour) | 0.031 (0.001) | +3.1% (0.1) | — |

   N = 413,732 engineer-weeks; 16,223 engineers; 43 weeks. The gradient is **monotonic with diminishing returns**, plateauing (not continuing to rise materially) from Moderate to High.

2. **Seven robustness/falsification tests — findings:**
   - **4.1 Placebo treatment (non-coding M365 Copilot in Word/Excel/PowerPoint/Teams/Outlook, measured as breadth):** gradient essentially flat across all usage levels — Low/Moderate marginally negative, High marginally positive (~+3%), CIs spanning or adjacent to zero. Contrasts sharply with the +40% GHCP-depth gradient. **Genuine null result** — the strongest single piece of evidence that this is a coding-tool-specific effect, not a generic "AI-engaged week" confound.
   - **4.2 Placebo outcome (teammates' PRs, leave-one-out):** individual GHCP usage predicts teammates' PRs at only **~1–2%**, vs 20–40% for the engineer's own PRs. **Genuine null result** — weighs against shared team-level shocks (sprint pressure, release pushes) as the confound.
   - **4.3 Task-mix shift (PR reviews given as outcome):** the reviews-given gradient is also monotonically *positive* across GHCP usage levels, rising alongside (not trading off against) the authored-PR gradient. This is the opposite of what a "reallocation from reviewing to authoring" story predicts; the authors note it does not rule out other task-mix shifts (e.g., toward qualitatively easier work) that wouldn't produce an authoring/reviewing tradeoff.
   - **4.4 Timing tests (lag t−1, lead t+1):** lagged usage coefficients near zero (no predictive power); leading usage coefficients slightly negative (consistent with mean reversion). Association is contemporaneous, not bleeding across week boundaries — a **genuine null** on cross-week contamination.
   - **4.5 PR size decomposition (files touched; ADO subsample: small=1 file, medium=2–6, large=7+):** dose-response is **steepest for large PRs**: +11% / +47% / +70% (Low/Moderate/High) for 7+-file PRs, vs small/medium gradients that rise then plateau or attenuate at High. Directly inconsistent with a "slicing work into more small PRs" explanation.
   - **4.6 PR-type decomposition (config/documentation-only vs. all other PRs; ADO subsample):** the "other PRs" (touching ≥1 source file) gradient is monotonic and *steeper* than the pooled headline — **+21% / +51% / +58%**. Because this excludes config/doc-only PRs by construction, the result is inconsistent with an "easier task mix" explanation. Exact numeric gradient for the config/doc-only series is not given in the prose (only shown graphically in Figure 9) — not reported here as a precise figure to avoid over-reading a chart.
   - **4.7 Alternative operationalisation (breadth instead of depth):** breadth-based measure (days/week with any usage) also produces a monotonic gradient, **High breadth = +54%** vs. zero-breadth weeks — converges qualitatively with the depth-based headline, suggesting the result is not an artefact of the bucketing choice.
   - Authors' own summary (§4.8): "No single test is decisive, but together they narrow the space of plausible alternative explanations." This is a candid, non-overclaiming synthesis sentence worth quoting directly if this source reaches synthesis.

3. **Heterogeneity — NOT tested, not a null result.** The paper reports **no breakdown whatsoever by seniority, tenure, level, or role.** The population is pre-restricted to individual contributors only (managers excluded from the study population entirely), and no within-IC heterogeneity (junior vs. senior, tenure bands) is estimated or reported anywhere in the text. **This is a gap in the evidence, not a finding of "no seniority effect"** — record it as such; do not conflate with he-2026-2x-mandate's explicit (and tested) flat-IC-to-Principal finding.

4. **No task-type (T1–T11) breakdown.** PRs are decomposed only by size (files touched) and by file-extension composition (config/doc vs. other) — proxies for task *complexity*, not the SDLC task-type taxonomy. Best approximate mapping: the whole study sits within **T4 (feature dev in a mature/production codebase)** given Microsoft Cloud+AI is a mature commercial org, but this is an inference by this extraction, not an author-assigned category, and the paper gives no way to separate T4 from T5/T6/T9/T11 within its PR counts.

5. **Quality/defect/rework — no data of any kind.** The outcome is PR *count* only (plus reviews-given and teammates'-PRs as auxiliary/placebo outcomes). **No revert rate, no defect rate, no incident data, no code-quality or maintainability measure appears anywhere in the paper.** This is a thinner instrument set than he-2026-2x-mandate (which at least reports coarse merge/revert proxies). Authors explicitly acknowledge PR count "captures only the activity and efficiency dimensions, leaving satisfaction, performance, and communication unmeasured" (§5.2, citing Forsgren et al.'s SPACE framework — direct cross-reference to the space-dx-core4 source already in this project's ledger).

6. **Cost data (RQ4) — none.** Zero cost figures of any kind: no licence cost, no token spend, no infrastructure cost, no review-overhead cost. Thinner than he-2026-2x-mandate's single Claude Code token-spend figure. Consistent with RQ4's pre-registered expectation of scarce cost data, even in a rigorous Tier A source.

## Authors' stated limitations (§5.2, in their own framing)

1. **Remaining threat / untestable assumption:** unobservable week-to-week shifts (qualitatively easier tasks beyond what file-count captures; intrinsic-motivation bursts beyond what coding-time captures) that could jointly drive GHCP use and PR output — precisely what Assumption 1 must rule out and cannot be directly tested.
2. **PR count as a narrow outcome:** developer productivity is "inherently multi-dimensional" (cites Forsgren et al. 2021, SPACE); this outcome captures only activity/efficiency, not satisfaction, performance, or communication.
3. **Contemporaneous measurement:** treatment and outcome measured in the same week; causal interpretation requires no residual same-week confounding after controls.
4. **PR-development lead time:** a PR created in week t may reflect work begun earlier; partially addressed by the null lag/lead timing tests and by the (counter-predicted) steepest gradient for large/long-running PRs, but not resolved at the individual-PR level.
5. **Efficiency estimand, not total effect:** conditioning on coding/browser time absorbs any time-savings channel; if GHCP does free up time, "the total productivity effect would exceed the efficiency effect reported here" — an explicit, self-flagged conservative/lower-bound framing.

## What this can and cannot support

**Can support (Tier A by design, E1/E2, strong-COI single-vendor-studying-own-tool):** that, within Microsoft's Cloud+AI organisation in 2025, weeks of heavier GitHub Copilot engagement are associated with a substantially higher rate of completed PRs per unit of measured coding/browser effort for the same engineer, with a monotonic, diminishing-returns dose-response shape that survives seven falsification tests including two genuine placebo tests. Can support the narrower point that the effect is not plausibly a generic "AI-engaged week" or team-level-shock artefact, is not concentrated in trivially small or config/doc-only PRs (if anything the opposite), and is contemporaneous rather than cross-week-contaminated.

**Cannot support:** any claim about the *agentic* era specifically — the telemetry does not confirm or separate out agent-mode/autonomous-agent usage, and the paper's own vocabulary ("suggestions, prompts, accepts") reads as autocomplete/chat telemetry; any claim about seniority/tenure heterogeneity in either direction (not tested, not reported); any claim about quality, defects, incidents, or technical debt (zero such measures exist in this paper); any claim about cost or ROI (zero cost data); any claim independent of the vendor-studying-itself COI (all three authors are Microsoft employees studying Microsoft's own product with no disclosed independent oversight, and no funding/COI statement exists in the text at all); and no causal point-estimate stronger than the authors' own explicit "rung 1... only a randomized experiment could close it definitively" framing — this is an unusually careful and honest paper about its own limits, and that honesty is itself part of what makes it usable, but the numbers should not be quoted without that caveat attached.

## Cross-ref guidance (for orchestrator — not written to contradictions.md)

**(a) vs. he-2026-2x-mandate:** He et al. describe this paper as "the within-engineer study closest to ours" — accurate for *design* (within-person fixed-effects dose-response on PR throughput) but not for *era* or *identification structure*. Differences worth carrying into synthesis:
- **Era:** he-2026-2x-mandate is confirmed E3 (Cursor, Claude Code, custom agents); Heilman et al. is E1/E2 (Copilot IDE "suggestions/prompts/accepts" telemetry, agentic content neither confirmed nor separable).
- **Identification:** he-2026-2x-mandate has a staggered-adoption DiD with a 113-strong never-adopter comparison group and a parallel-trends event-study check; Heilman et al. has no adoption-event structure at all — pure intensity variation within an always-adopter population, with two placebo tests as its main causal-robustness lever instead.
- **Magnitudes, broadly consistent despite the differences:** Heilman's headline (+40.5% at max dose) sits almost exactly on he-2026-2x-mandate's *pooled single-coefficient* estimate (+42%, their Table I) and below their within-developer causal decomposition (+46–72%, i.e. 1.46–1.72×) and well below their raw aggregate pre/post ratio (2.09×). The two studies' numbers occupy the same rough order of magnitude (+20% to +70% depending which internal estimate is compared to which), but given the era and design differences this should be read as **broadly consistent, not a strict replication** — the same caution he-2026-2x-mandate itself applies when comparing to the Stanford quadrant.
- **Quality/cost instrumentation:** he-2026-2x-mandate at least has coarse merge/revert proxies and one cost figure; Heilman et al. has neither — it is the thinner of the two on everything except the causal-throughput headline itself.

**(b) vs. cui-2024-copilot-field (seniority pattern — does dose-response at Microsoft in the "agentic era" confirm or reverse the junior-gains pattern?):** **It does neither — this paper is silent on seniority.** No tenure/level/role breakdown is reported anywhere; the population is IC-only with no within-IC heterogeneity tested. This cannot be read as evidence against the Cui et al. junior-gains pattern, nor as confirmation of he-2026-2x-mandate's flat-IC-to-Principal finding — it is simply a gap. Compounding this, the era mismatch (E1/E2 here vs. Cui et al.'s own E1 tag and he-2026-2x-mandate's E3) means that even if a seniority split existed, it would sit at a different point on the era-transfer question than the one the research programme most wants answered (does the agentic era specifically flatten or reverse the junior-gains pattern?). Recommend the orchestrator continue treating this as an open question resting on he-2026-2x-mandate (flat, E3, single firm) versus Cui et al./Microsoft-Accenture (junior-favouring, E1) with no third E3-era data point yet from this source.

**(c) vs. DX/Faros throughput magnitudes:** not independently checked in this task (would require reading dora-2026-roi and faros-2026-whiplash primary content, which is out of this task's remit) — flagging for the orchestrator to cross-check magnitude only. Qualitatively, this paper's dose-response shape (steep rise then plateau, ceiling around +40%) is a clean, well-identified anchor point for evaluating whether any DX/Faros-published customer PR-throughput numbers (which the ledger already grades D for vendor-publication reasons) sit within a plausible range or look inflated relative to a source with genuine falsification testing behind it.
