# Source: linearb-2026-benchmarks

## Identity
- Title: LinearB, "2026 Software Engineering Benchmarks Report: The AI productivity edition" (48 pp.; author voice Yishai Beeri, CTO LinearB). PDF metadata creation date 2025-12-09.
- URLs: https://assets.linearb.io/image/upload/v1765300694/resources/LinearB_2026_Software_Engineering_Benchmarks_Report.pdf and the earlier-upload URL (v1764965485). **Both URLs return byte-identical files (same MD5, 1,236,283 bytes); there is no version difference to record.**
- Companion pages read for the inconsistency check: linearb.io/blog/engineering-benchmarks-report, linearb.io/engineering-benchmarks/, linearb.io/resources/software-engineering-benchmarks-report (landing summaries) and linearb.io/library/ai-in-software-development (HTML restatement with tables).
- Read depth: FULL report text (all 48 pages). The PDF has no standard text layer for much of its content; text was recovered by decoding the content streams (glyph-ID encoding) and chart labels were read as text. Chart geometry (e.g. the adoption-vs-acceptance scatter and the monthly tool lines) was not recoverable; only labelled values and surrounding prose are used. Companion pages were read through a summarising fetch, so their figures are second-hand to that extent.
- Vendor: LinearB (software-engineering-intelligence platform). Published ~Dec 2025; data window for the AI-type cut is Jan-Sep 2025 (monthly chart); survey fielded Nov 2025.

## What this document is
A vendor benchmark report (fifth annual edition) with: (a) P75 benchmark tiers for 20 delivery, predictability and project-management metrics, by all orgs and by org size; (b) a new acceptance-rate benchmark; (c) an AI section comparing three PR types (agentic AI, AI-assisted, unassisted) on size, pickup, review, acceptance and refactor rate; (d) results of LinearB's own "2026 AI in Engineering Leadership Survey" (adoption, confidence, measurement, readiness). It is also a product pitch (AI code review, workflow automation, ROI reporting, "prove if AI is increasing" velocity).

## Full notes
**Dataset.** 8,109,244 PRs; 4,813 teams (cover says "4,800"); 163,820 active contributors; 42 countries. Org size mix: start-up (0-200) 59.7%, scale-up (200-1000) 24.4%, enterprise (1000+) 15.8%. Region split printed as 57.3% / 25.8% / 16.9% across North America / Europe / rest of world (label-to-value mapping is ambiguous in the extraction). Data "anonymised and normalised"; aggregation is P75 per team. Tier bands are relative ranks of included orgs: elite = top 10%, good = top 30%, fair = top 60%, needs focus = bottom 40%. The website says "4,800+ organizations" in one place and "4,800 engineering teams" in another; the report uses teams.

**Classification.** Definitions are conceptual only. Agentic AI PRs: created by AI agents (Devin, Copilot Coding Agent, OpenAI Codex "and others") that take a task (directly or from a Jira ticket), generate code, commit and open the PR, without human authorship; the report distinguishes these from earlier non-AI bot PRs (Dependabot, Renovate, Snyk). AI-assisted PRs: "authored or significantly shaped with the help of AI-powered development tools", human intent and ownership retained. Unassisted: "authored entirely by developers without substantial use of AI tools". **The detection mechanism is not disclosed for any class** (bot accounts, commit trailers, IDE telemetry or an internal classifier). For AI-assisted PRs in particular, how "significantly shaped" is determined is unstated; the report's remark that "AI tools ... create commits for developers" hints at tool-generated commit signals but does not say so. Bot-tool series (Copilot, Devin) imply author-account identification for agentic PRs, as inference. No per-class PR or team counts are given.

Insight headlines: "AI-assisted PRs are 2.6x larger than unassisted" (408/157 = 2.6; note it is AI-assisted, not agentic, and the accompanying prose partly blurs this); "Agentic AI PRs have a PR pickup time 5.3x longer" (1,055/201 = 5.25); "Acceptance rate for AI PRs is less than half that of manual" (32.7% vs 84.4%); "Heavier agentic AI adoption is not translating to greater value delivery"; "AI PR 30-day acceptance varies widely by tool"; "AI use is biased towards new code, rather than refactoring".

**Pickup-time discrepancy (reconciliation).** Inside the PDF the figure is 5.3x (chart title and insight) from 1,055 vs 201 minutes = 5.25x; the library page restates it as "5.25x gap" with 17.6 h vs 3.4 h (which gives 5.18x because of the rounded denominator). All three are the same data, rounding differences only. The blog and landing page additionally say "AI PRs wait 4.6x longer before review" with no class or denominator; **4.6x does not appear anywhere in the PDF and cannot be derived from it** (AI-assisted/unassisted is 2.5x; agentic/unassisted 5.25x). Unreconciled; treat 4.6x as unsourced marketing copy, possibly from a pre-publication cut. Use 5.25x (agentic, P75) as the report figure.

**"Reviewed 2x faster once picked up".** Not in the PDF. The PDF shows AI-assisted reviewed in 194 vs 252 min (about 1.3x faster, -23%) and agentic reviewed in 382 min, i.e. 1.5x SLOWER than unassisted. The report's prose says AI PRs are "reviewed in significantly less time" and elsewhere agentic review is "somewhat slower". The landing-page 2x claim is unsupported by, and in part contradicts, the report. The report interprets fast review as shallow scanning, citing survey quotes about rubber-stamping (not measured).

**Acceptance.** Defined as share of PRs merged within 30 days of creation (or leaving draft); vendor says the insight holds at 7d, 14d and unlimited thresholds. Pooled: 32.7% (AI PRs) vs 84.4% (manual). The report does not state whether "AI PRs" in the headline is agentic only, agentic plus assisted, or all bot-authored; the benchmark tiers (below) are for agentic PRs, and the monthly chart tracks Copilot and Devin bot accounts against manual (manual about 80-82% month to month, slightly below the 84.4% headline). Benchmark tiers: all PRs elite >95%, good 91-95%, fair 85-90%, needs focus <85%; manual >95 / 92-95 / 87-91 / <87; agentic >71 / 61-71 / 42-60 / <42. Report: "most teams find it hard to exceed 60%" for AI PRs. Vendor explanations (all untested): no human owner, agentic flows pointed at low-priority backlog, larger PRs, reviewer hesitancy. Tool series (read from chart/prose, Jan-Sep 2025): Devin improving from April toward human baseline; Copilot declining from May. One outlier org running Devin at very high adoption has near-parity acceptance.

**Adoption vs outcome.** Insight 04 is an org-level scatter of AI-PR share against acceptance (both AI and non-AI series). Prose: higher-adoption orgs "rarely outperform" lower; no numeric deltas; adoption "alone isn't the driver ... process maturity, tooling and reviewer confidence appear to matter far more" (assertion, not tested). The widely circulated "+98% PRs merged, +91% review time, +154% PR size" for heavy-adoption teams is NOT in this report; those numbers, as I recall, come from Faros AI's 2025 productivity-paradox study (unverified here; check provenance before any use). The "1.7x more issues per PR" is likewise absent; as I recall it is from CodeRabbit's AI-vs-human code report (unverified here). A cycle-time-share-in-review figure is also absent; only review/pickup/approve/merge times are benchmarked.

**Quality / defect / incident outcomes.** None measured by PR type. The only quality-adjacent PR-type metrics are refactor rate and rework rate (code-churn proxies, 21-day threshold). Change failure rate is benchmarked (elite <1%, good 1-4%, fair 5-17%, needs focus >17%; org-size bands differ) but not split by AI class; MTTR was removed from the benchmarks because definitions vary too much. Quality claims about AI code rest on survey quotes only.

**Benchmark tiers (all orgs, P75, hours).** Coding <54 min / 54 min-4 / 5-23 / >23; pickup <1 / 1-4 / 5-16 / >16; approve <10 / 10-22 / 23-42 / >42; merge <1 / 1-3 / 4-16 / >16; review <3 / 3-14 / 15-24 / >24; deploy <16 / 16-106 / 107-277 / >277; cycle <25 / 25-72 / 73-161 / >161; merge frequency >2.0 / 2-1.2 / 1.2-0.66 / <0.66 per dev per week; PR size <100 / 100-155 / 156-228 / >228; PR maturity >89% / 89-83 / 82-77 / <77; CFR as above; refactor <11% / 11-16 / 17-22 / >22; rework <3 / 3-5 / 6-8 / >8. Pickup is "time a PR waits for someone to start reviewing"; review time is "to complete a code review and get a PR merged"; cycle time runs "code to production". Note the 17.6 h agentic P75 would sit in "needs focus" (>16 h) and unassisted 3.35 h in "good". Enterprise, scale-up and start-up pickup bands: <2 / 2-4 / 5-13 / >13; <2 / 2-6 / 7-16 / >16; <2 / 2-5 / 6-17 / >17.

**Comparison design.** Cross-sectional across PRs and orgs, pooled; no within-team before/after, no matching, no control for task type, repo, team seniority or PR priority. The report itself concedes the key confounds (no owner, low-priority backlog work). The disclaimer states correlation is not causation.

**Survey (separate instrument).** Fielded Nov 2025; n not stated (about 94 implied by percentage granularity). Daily AI use 64.9% (text) vs 63.5% (chart); 88.3% use daily or several times a week (text); adoption "71.6% in Jan 2024 to 88.3% in 2025" compares two different questions and samples (the Jan 2024 figure counts any stage from trial to full deployment). 76.1% report some or significant measurable improvement in coding speed or output; 44.7% do not measure AI impact formally; confidence in AI code quality 6.4% extremely, 39.4% somewhat, 33% neutral, 19.1% somewhat unconfident, 2.1% not at all. Readiness matrix (7 areas, modelled on DORA): data not AI-ready 64.5% (not strongly agree), clear policy 30.5% strongly agree vs 21.1% strongly disagree. Report text itself notes the productivity gains are "based on anecdotal observations".

## Tier & era grading
- **Tier D** (vendor telemetry, vendor survey; reverse-direction COI). LinearB sells engineering metrics, AI code review, PR automation/policy gating and "executive ROI reporting"; a narrative of "AI PRs are slow, large, low-trust, and you need governance and measurement" sells the product. Population is LinearB's customer base (start-up weighted) with unknown selection. Not Tier B despite scale: no counterfactual, undisclosed classification, pooled P75.
- **Era:** agentic-PR rows are **E3** if (and only if) "agentic" means agent-authored PRs (Devin, Copilot Coding Agent, Codex), which is what the definition says, though the detection method is not disclosed. AI-assisted and unassisted rows are **unsplit** (tool modality of "assisted" unknown; likely a mix of E1/E2/E3-IDE). Survey rows unsplit. Data window Jan-Sep 2025.

## Measured vs self-reported
- Measured (vendor telemetry): PR sizes, pickup, review, acceptance, refactor/rework, tiers.
- Self-reported: all survey items (adoption, confidence, perceived gains, measurement practice, readiness) and all the "what leaders are saying" quotes.
- Delta as its own finding: 76.1% of surveyed leaders report measurable gains in speed/output, while measured PR data show agentic PRs waiting 5.25x longer and merging at 32.7% vs 84.4%; the report notes that 44.7% do not measure formally. The two are different populations and different units (leader perception vs PR flow), so this is a juxtaposition, not a computed gap.

## Can / cannot support - verdicts
- Can support (directional, Tier D): bottleneck-displacement signal (agent-authored PRs queue for review, larger diffs, low merge rate); the point that agentic output only counts when merged and that ownership is a mechanism hypothesis; the hype-baseline observation that vendor pages cite stronger numbers than the full report; RQ6 material on pickup time, acceptance rate and PR size as instrumentable metrics.
- Cannot support: any claim of AI quality or defect effect (nothing measured); any productivity or throughput effect (no outcome per team over time); any "heavy adoption" delta; 4.6x and 2x-faster claims; causal reading of the acceptance gap (ownership, priority and PR-size confounds acknowledged by the vendor); the +98%/+91%/+154% and 1.7x issues-per-PR figures (not in this source). Cannot carry an exec-brief claim alone (Tier D).

## Red flags / things the orchestrator should know
1. **Self-inconsistency on pickup time:** 5.3x (caption/PDF; 1,055 vs 201 min = 5.25x) vs 4.6x (blog/landing, not in the PDF, no stated class or denominator) vs 17.6 h / 3.4 h = 5.18x (library page rounding). All the in-report numbers agree; 4.6x is unreconciled and should not be cited.
2. **"Reviewed 2x faster" is not in the report;** the PDF shows AI-assisted 1.3x faster and agentic 1.5x slower. Do not cite the web claim.
3. **408 vs 157 is AI-assisted vs unassisted** (2.6x), not agentic; agentic is 293 vs 157 (1.9x). Secondary summaries blur this.
4. **Cross-sectional selection:** PR-level and org-level comparisons, no within-team change, no matching; agentic flows are aimed at low-priority backlog work per the vendor. The adoption analysis is between orgs.
5. **"AI PRs" in the 32.7% vs 84.4% headline is undefined** (agentic only? all bot-authored? includes AI-assisted?) and the headline manual rate (84.4%) differs from the chart (about 80-82%). Possible right-censoring from the 30-day window is not discussed.
6. **No quality outcome:** "issues" per PR is not measured anywhere in the report; the 1.7x figure and the heavy-adoption deltas (+98%, +91%, +154%) are not in this source and, as I recall, originate elsewhere (Faros AI; CodeRabbit). Treat as misattributed until traced.
7. **Classification undisclosed;** survey internal figure mismatches (64.9 vs 63.5, 23.4 vs 22.9, "45%/75%" vs 44.7/76.1); survey n not given.
8. Pooled P75 only; no confidence intervals; the tiers are relative ranks, not validated outcomes.
9. COI: vendor sells the remedy (AI code review, automation, ROI reporting).
