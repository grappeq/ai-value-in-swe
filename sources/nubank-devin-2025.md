## Scope and nature of the work (task-type mapping)

Migration of Nubank's **core ETL system** — described as an 8-year-old, multi-million-line-of-code (**6M+ LOC**) monolith, born from a "centralised ETL FinServ architecture" — into smaller, more flexible sub-modules. Drivers cited: customer growth, geographic expansion (Brazil/Mexico/Colombia), product expansion beyond the original credit-card business, and dependency chains **up to 70 deep**. The concrete unit of work was moving **~100,000 data class implementations** across the new module boundaries (import tracing, refactoring, edge-case handling — e.g. detecting a data class's country extension, `br`/`co`/`mx`). Original scoping: **18 months, 1,000+ engineers**. Actual outcome as reported: unspecified business units (Data, Collections, Risk, named in (a) only) "verified and completed their migrations in weeks."

- **Primary task-type code: T5 (refactoring / tech debt — migration).** This is a mechanically-flavoured, well-specified transformation (move/rewire data classes across module boundaries) rather than open-ended design work, similar in character to the Google migration case studies reviewed elsewhere in this research (see `sources/google-migrations-2025.md`).
- **Cross-cutting context tag: brownfield, mature, high-implicit-context codebase (T4-adjacent)** — an 8-year-old monolith with 70-deep dependency chains is exactly the "contested zone" codebase profile the protocol's T4 notes describe, even though the work itself (T5) is more mechanical than typical T4 feature development.
- **Tooling / era:** Devin is described in both artefacts as an autonomous agent that engineers "delegate" tasks to, which works with only human review/merge at the end — multi-file, plan-execute-verify, PR-generating. **Confirmed E3 (agentic era) by modality**, consistent with §2. Both write-ups describe the same tool and the same era; there is no E1/E2 dimension to this record.

## How the counterfactual was constructed

Neither artefact describes a designed counterfactual (no A/B, no matched-task comparison, no randomisation). The two headline multipliers are each built from a single-sided estimate compared against an unspecified "typical" or historical baseline:
- **Efficiency (8-12x):** typical engineering hours for "a data class migration task" (source and sample size unstated) vs. actual hours spent prompting/reviewing Devin on "the same task" (also not described as a matched pair — likely an average-vs-average comparison, not a per-task paired measurement).
- **Cost (20x+):** Devin's run cost vs. an average engineer's hourly rate, applied to the same task unit — a unit-economics ratio, not a total-cost-of-project comparison, and explicitly excluding schedule-acceleration value by the vendor's own admission.
- The original **18-month/1,000-engineer estimate** functions as the implicit "what it would have cost without the agent" counterfactual for the whole-project framing, but its provenance (whose estimate, when made, on what basis) is not documented in either artefact.

## Tier, era, independence, COI — graded separately per artefact

- **(a) Vendor customer-story page — Tier D (vendor & consultancy).** Per §3, this is exactly the "GitHub/Microsoft/Anthropic/McKinsey-class" vendor-publication category. **COI: HIGH, and flagged explicitly.** Cognition/Devin has a direct commercial interest in maximising the multipliers it publishes about its own product; the only methodology disclosed is one sentence per figure, with no raw data, no external audit, and a curated customer quote. **Independence: none** — self-published by the tool's maker about its own tool.
- **(b) Nubank engineering blog — Tier C (engineering-org case study / credible engineering blog).** Per §3 this is the protocol's expected volume centre for practitioner evidence. **COI: MEDIUM, flagged.** Not a vendor, but a self-reported success story about the org's own initiative, with clear employer-branding/PR incentive, no raw data, no external verification, and (per the version-diff above) apparently reliant on the same unaudited figures the vendor also uses. **Independence: not fully independent from (a)** — same underlying rollout, likely same original source narrative (the Clojure Conj 2024 talk), overlapping named engineers; **this pair should be counted as one organisation / one rollout, not two corroborating Tier C+D sources**, consistent with how the Google migration pair elsewhere in this research (`sources/google-migrations-2025.md`) is treated for the §3 triangulation rule.
- **Era: E3 for both artefacts**, confirmed by tooling modality (autonomous, multi-step, plan-execute-verify agent with PR generation and human-only-at-merge review) — no E1/E2 ambiguity here.

## Limitations and what the artefacts do NOT report

- **No defect rate, bug count, or production-incident data** for the AI-migrated code, in either artefact.
- **No description of QA/testing process**, beyond the single vendor-page customer quote: "Engineers reviewed changes, made minor adjustments, then merged PRs." Nubank's own blog says nothing at all about review or verification process for this migration.
- **No resolution of completion scope** — whether the full ~100,000 data-class migration was actually finished via Devin, or only a subset (the three named business units), is never clarified in either artefact.
- **No absolute hours or dollar figures** — everything is presented as a ratio/multiplier, so the actual magnitude of hours or money saved cannot be recovered from either artefact.
- **No sample size, variance, or confidence interval** for the "typical engineering hours" baseline used to derive the 8-12x and 20x figures.
- **No rework/revert/failure-rate data** — contrast with the Google migration papers reviewed elsewhere in this research corpus, which explicitly disclose human revert/rework rates (25.55%/30.54% human-fallback share) and a "quality impact remains to be seen" caveat; neither Nubank artefact discloses anything comparable.
- **No long-term quality, maintainability, or stability assessment** of the migrated codebase post-rollout.
- **No statistical treatment whatsoever** — every figure is a bare point estimate.
- **Vendor page carries no publication date**, which blocks establishing a clean timeline against the dated blog post and against the (likely earlier) Clojure Conj 2024 talk.
- **The fine-tuning score-doubling/4x-speed figures are eval-set results, not production measurements** — this distinction is easy to lose (and is lost in most secondary reporting/social-media circulation of this case) when the figure is repeated alongside the production-flavoured 8-12x/20x claims.

## What this record can and cannot support

**Can support:** (1) a Tier D-anchored, Tier C-echoed **directional** signal that agentic tooling (Devin, E3) can compress a large, well-scoped, mechanical migration task (T5, brownfield/legacy context) — consistent with the general T5-favourable pattern seen in the independently-sourced Google migration case studies; (2) a clear illustration for RQ2 of how vendor case studies inflate and multiply figures relative to the named organisation's own, more conservative account — useful as a **methodology exemplar of the vendor-vs-practitioner reporting gap**, not as a value-map data point in its own right; (3) a worked example for RQ6 of why headline multipliers ("20x cost savings") should not be taken at face value without checking whether the org itself corroborates them.

**Cannot support:** (1) any claim resting on the 20x cost-savings figure, the 8x/8-12x range, the fine-tuning score/speed figures, or the 70-deep/100k-data-class specifics as if they were confirmed by the named customer — Nubank's own words corroborate only the "~12-fold efficiency" figure and vague "cost savings" language; (2) any quality, defect-rate, or long-term stability claim — neither artefact reports this; (3) any exec-brief claim on its own, given the Tier D origin of nearly all the granular numbers and the non-independence between (a) and (b); per the §3 triangulation rule this pair counts as **one** data point, and per DoD (§8) no claim may rest on Tier D alone; (4) any claim that this was a measured, counterfactual-based result — both headline multipliers are estimate-vs-baseline ratios with no described controls, sampling, or variance.
