# dora-2025 — DORA State of AI-assisted Software Development 2025

## Citation

DORA (Google Cloud). **"State of AI-assisted Software Development 2025"** (the 2025 DORA Report). Published 23 September 2025; current document version **v2025.2**. 142 pages.

- Canonical landing page: https://dora.dev/dora-report-2025/
- Full report PDF (public, no gate): https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/2025_state_of_ai_assisted_software_development.pdf
- Announcement: https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/announcing-the-2025-dora-report (23 Sep 2025)
- Companion publication: "DORA AI Capabilities Model" guide — https://cloud.google.com/resources/content/2025-dora-ai-capabilities-model-report

## Verification status

**VERIFIED.** Report exists, is publicly downloadable (no registration gate), and the full text (142 pp) was fetched and read directly. All figures below are extracted from the PDF (v2025.2) unless otherwise marked. One caveat: the headline effect estimates (Figure 28) are published as a chart of standardised betas with 89% credible intervals; the report body does **not** print the numeric coefficients, so only direction and approximate magnitude (chart axis spans −0.05 to +0.20 standardised) can be recorded. The report does not publish a 2025 analogue of 2024's "per 25% increase in AI adoption" percentage effects.

## Tier, era, independence

- **Tier: B** — large-N (4,867) cross-sectional industry survey with structured Bayesian analysis; explicitly the "DORA-class longitudinal industry report" named in PROTOCOL §3 as the Tier B exemplar. Carries claims with caveats.
- **Era: mixed E1/E2/E3, weighted E1/E2.** Tagged by modality actually studied (survey fielded 13 Jun–21 Jul 2025): respondents report chat (E2) and predictive text (E1) as the most common interaction modes; **61% report *never* using agent mode** (E3), and "collaborative" broad-change mode is also minority use. The AI-adoption factor aggregates all modalities, so outcome findings **cannot be split by era** — treat the throughput/instability findings as predominantly pre-agentic-usage evidence with a minority E3 component, not as E3 evidence.
- **Independence / COI:** published by Google Cloud, a vendor of AI developer tooling (Gemini/Code Assist); sponsored by GitHub and GitLab (both AI-tool vendors). Not graded Tier D because DORA maintains an academic-style, methodology-transparent research programme, but a **vendor-adjacency flag applies**: the publisher and sponsors all benefit commercially from AI adoption. Population is self-selected survey respondents (convenience sample), not a random sample.

## Method & population

- Global online survey, fielded **13 June – 21 July 2025**; **n = 4,867** respondents ("nearly 5,000 technology professionals"), plus **100+ hours of qualitative data** (interviews).
- Sample: median age 41; 62.8% men, 19.2% women; median 6 years in role, 3 years on current team; median 16 months of AI-tool experience (median adoption start April 2024).
- Analysis: exploratory factor analysis to build an "AI adoption" factor from three items (reliance, trust, reflexive use); Bayesian regression reporting standardised beta weights with **89% credible intervals**; team profiles from cluster analysis.
- **Measurement basis: all outcomes are survey-based self-report** — including "software delivery throughput" and "software delivery instability", which are respondent-reported, not telemetry (RQ2-relevant). The report is explicit about causal limits (footnote 20): "we will speak in terms of comparisons… ultimately, we're doing comparisons", not effects.

## Key findings (exact as reported; all self-reported unless noted)

Adoption and use (chapter "AI adoption and use"):
- **90%** of respondents use AI at work, "a 14.1% increase over the same metric in last year's report". [self-reported]
- Median **2 hours** of the most recent workday spent interacting with AI ("about one-quarter of an eight-hour workday"). [self-reported]
- Top task: **writing new code — 71%** of respondents who write code use AI for it [T3/T4]; modifying existing code 66% [T4/T5]; literature reviews 68%; proofreading 66%; creating/editing images 66%; analysing requirements 49% [T1]; internal communication 48%; calendar management 25%. (Task list also covers code review [T7], debugging [T8], creating test cases [T6], writing documentation [T10], maintaining legacy code [T5], security/performance analysis [T8/T11].) [self-reported]
- Interaction modes: chat and predictive text most common; **agent mode "never" used by 61%** of respondents. Surfaces: conversational chatbots most common, then AI embedded in the IDE. [self-reported]
- Reliance: 65% rely on AI a "moderate amount" (37%), "a lot" (20%) or "a great deal" (8%); only 5% "not at all". [self-reported]

Perceived impact:
- **>80%** report AI increased their productivity (41% "slightly increased", 31% "moderately", 13% "extremely"); fewer than 10% perceive any decrease (5% total decreased; 9% no impact). [self-reported, perception]
- **59%** observe positive impact on code quality (31% slightly, 21% moderately, 7% extremely improved); 30% no impact; **10%** perceive worsening. [self-reported, perception]
- Trust in AI-generated output: 70% express some degree of confidence; **24%** "a great deal" (4%) or "a lot" (20%); 46% "somewhat"; **30%** "a little" (23%) or "not at all" (7%). [self-reported]

Outcome associations (Figure 28, standardised betas, 89% credible intervals; all outcomes self-reported):
- Higher AI adoption is associated with **higher**: individual effectiveness, software delivery **throughput**, organisational performance, valuable work time, code quality, product performance, team performance — and **higher software delivery instability** (undesirable).
- **No relationship** with friction or burnout (nulls, recorded per protocol).
- Numeric betas are chart-encoded only (axis −0.05 to +0.20 standardised); not printed in text.
- **Shifts vs 2024, as stated:** "AI's relationship with valuable time has reversed from negative to positive; AI's relationship with software delivery throughput has turned from negative to positive; AI's relationship with product performance has shifted from neutral to positive." Instability remains adverse.
- 2024 baseline quoted within this report: DORA 2024 "found an estimated **1.5% reduction in software delivery throughput** and an estimated **7.2% increase in software delivery instability** for every 25% increase in AI adoption."
- Trade-off check: the report tested whether AI adoption moderates (blunts) instability's harms and "found no evidence of such a moderating effect… instability still has significant detrimental effects on crucial outcomes like product performance and burnout, which can ultimately negate any perceived gains in throughput." (RQ5-relevant.)

Framing and models:
- Amplifier thesis (executive summary): "AI's primary role in software development is that of an amplifier. It magnifies the strengths of high-performing organizations and the dysfunctions of struggling ones." And: "Without this foundation, AI creates localized pockets of productivity that are often lost to downstream chaos."
- **Seven team profiles** (cluster analysis, % of respondents): Foundational challenges (10%), Legacy bottleneck (11%), Constrained by process (17%), High impact, low cadence (7%), Stable and methodical (15%), Pragmatic performers (20%), Harmonious high-achievers (20%).
- **DORA AI Capabilities Model — seven capabilities** shown to amplify AI's benefits: clear and communicated AI stance; working in small batches; AI-accessible internal data; quality internal platforms; healthy data ecosystems; user-centric focus; strong version control practices.
- **90% of organisations have adopted platform engineering**; internal platform quality gates AI's effect on organisational performance (negligible when platform quality is low, strong and positive when high — per report and announcement).
- Value stream management is reported to act as a force multiplier for AI (valuable-work time).
- The report itself cites METR 2025 (−19% actual vs +20% perceived) as third-party evidence of the perception–reality gap — DORA acknowledges the tension with its own self-report basis (RQ2/RQ6-relevant).

Limitations (as stated or evident):
- Cross-sectional snapshot; no panel data; comparisons not causal effects (footnote 20 is unusually candid).
- All outcomes self-reported, including delivery metrics; convenience sample; vendor-adjacent publisher and sponsors.
- Adoption factor bundles use, reliance, and trust — findings are about that composite, not tool hours or any specific tool/modality.

## What this can and cannot support

Can support (with Tier B caveats): directional claims that AI adoption is now associated with self-reported throughput gains but persistent delivery instability, that organisational context moderates AI's value, and adoption/trust base rates. Cannot support: any measured (telemetry) effect size, causal claims, or E3/agentic-specific claims — agent-mode use was a minority behaviour in this sample, and all outcomes are self-reported.

## Corrections vs the seed description (PROTOCOL §5.3 / §6)

1. Seed's "amplifier" framing is **accurate** — it is the report's own central thesis, verbatim.
2. Seed undersells one point: DORA 2025 is not merely a *reframe* of the 2024 finding — it reports a **directional flip**: the AI-adoption/throughput association turned from negative (2024) to positive (2025), while the instability association persisted. Both halves belong in the §5.3 tension record.
3. Title: "State of AI-assisted Software Development 2025", not "State of DevOps 2025". Current version v2025.2.
4. Precise 2024 anchors (quoted in the 2025 report): −1.5% throughput, +7.2% instability per 25% increase in AI adoption — useful when the DORA 2024 source record is written.
