## What this study actually is (lab/benchmark, not field data)

This is a **controlled benchmark study of LLM reasoning capability**, not a field study of incident response outcomes. Critical framing points:

1. **Synthetic fault injection on small open-source demo systems**, not production incidents. Dataset A = MicroSS (from the GAIA/"Generic AIOps Atlas" benchmark), 24 entities, 152 fault samples after filtering/balancing (from 16,205 raw injected faults downsampled/augmented — see Appendix A.2). Dataset B = "Market" from OpenRCA, built on Google's Online Boutique demo microservice app, 60 entities, 148 samples. These are widely-used **academic RCA benchmarks**, not live enterprise telemetry.
2. **Six open-source, non-frontier LLMs only**: Llama 3.2 (3B), Qwen 3 (4B and 32B), Llama 3.3 Instruct (70B), Command R+ (104B), and a distilled DeepSeek-R1 (70B, tool-calling unsupported so restricted to the non-agentic condition). **No GPT-4/5, Claude, Gemini, or other frontier proprietary model was tested** — the authors explicitly restrict to open-source models "suitable for in-house execution" for privacy/security reasons. This means the study says nothing directly about the agentic coding/ops tools we actually care about (Claude Code, Cursor, Devin-class); it is a lower-capability-bound study.
3. **Deliberately simplified/artificial environment**: alert-level inputs (not raw telemetry), an explicit typed knowledge graph provided to the agent, minimal deterministic tools (no code execution, no direct KG access), single-fault-only scenarios (no concurrent/overlapping faults). The authors are explicit that this isolates "reasoning" from confounds like multi-agent orchestration — a deliberate trade-off against realism.
4. **No human RCA baseline or human-expert comparison anywhere in the study.** The only baseline is a random-guessing statistical floor computed from the number of fault-location/type categories. This is a significant absence for our purposes — the source cannot speak to "AI vs human" debugging speed/accuracy, only to internal LLM/workflow comparisons and a floor of chance.
5. **"48,000 simulated failure scenarios, 228 days of execution time"** is confirmed exactly as stated in the brief — but this is total **compute wall-clock time across 10×RTX A6000 + 8×V100 GPUs**, not evidence of cost/effort at production scale. It is a proxy for computational cost of agentic vs non-agentic workflows (see cost finding below), not a licence/token-spend figure.

**Framing rule applied throughout this extraction: findings below describe LLM reasoning failure modes and capability ceilings in a controlled benchmark, not measured field outcomes of AI-assisted incident response. They may support RQ3 (failure map / task characteristics predicting AI failure) as mechanism evidence, and may inform RQ6 (what NOT to assume about agentic reliability), but must not be used to make claims about human debugging productivity, MTTR, or field incident outcomes (RQ1, RQ5).**

## Reasoning Failure Taxonomy (16 categories, exact per Table 1)

Organised into three buckets by the authors: **General** (credibility/logic defects), **RCA-specific** (domain diagnostic errors), **Procedural** (agent decision-loop breakdowns). Scope column: per-hypothesis / full trace / cross-cutting.

| ID | Name | Category | Scope | Description (verbatim, condensed) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| RF-01 | Fabricated evidence | General | Per-hypothesis | Asserts alerts/metrics/logs/traces not found in provided evidence |
| RF-02 | Metric interpretation error | RCA-specific | Per-hypothesis | Misreads metric semantics (inverts direction, confuses counters/gauges) |
| RF-03 | Confused provenance | RCA-specific | Per-hypothesis | Blames the component observing a symptom rather than its true source |
| RF-04 | Temporal misordering | General | Per-hypothesis | Causal direction violates chronological order of events |
| RF-05 | Spurious causal attribution | General | Per-hypothesis | Causal claims unsupported by alerts/KG structure |
| RF-06 | Unjustified instance specificity | RCA-specific | Per-hypothesis | Instance-level fault claimed without instance-discriminating evidence |
| RF-07 | Arbitrary evidence selection | RCA-specific | Per-hypothesis | Evidence subset chosen inconsistent with systematic triage heuristics |
| RF-08 | Evidential insufficiency | General | Per-hypothesis | Weak/non-specific evidence insufficient to support the claim |
| RF-09 | Failure to update belief | Procedural | Full trace | Does not revise claims contradicted by later evidence/tool output |
| RF-10 | Simulation or role confusion | Procedural | Full trace | Treats simulated/assumed tool outputs as factual evidence |
| RF-11 | Excessive speculation | Procedural | Full trace | Prolonged speculative/circular reasoning obstructing analysis |
| RF-12 | Repetition or failure to resume | Procedural | Full trace | Repeats planning/reasoning across turns without progress |
| RF-13 | Anchoring bias | General | Cross-cutting | Fixates prematurely on one hypothesis, neglects alternatives |
| RF-14 | Invalid inference pattern | General | Cross-cutting | Formal/informal logical fallacies in diagnostic reasoning |
| RF-15 | Internal contradiction | General | Cross-cutting | Mutually inconsistent statements within the inference history |
| RF-16 | Arithmetic or aggregation error | Procedural | Cross-cutting | Numeric miscalculations affecting interpretation |

**Taxonomy provenance:** built via mixed-methods open coding on 30 initial samples, refined over 7 iterative rounds of paired LLM-judge + human annotation (20 samples/round), reliability-checked with Cohen's κ = 0.92 (95% CI) on a held-out 30-sample human review. Taxonomy induced from only 170 total qualitatively-coded samples — authors themselves flag this may miss rare failure types (internal validity limitation, self-reported).

**Important gap vs the brief: the paper does NOT publish a single table of exact taxonomy-wide frequencies/percentages.** Per-RF prevalence is shown only as dot plots (Figure 4, "proportion flagged" 0–1 scale per model×workflow) and is discussed qualitatively in the text (Observations 8–11 below) — I cannot extract exact percentage figures per RF from the rendered PDF, only the relative/qualitative patterns the authors state explicitly, plus the specific quantitative call-outs they give in prose (quoted below). Do not treat this record as containing a full frequency table — it does not exist in exact numeric form in the paper's own text (charts only).

## Findings (RQ1–RQ4)

**RQ1 — accuracy.** Metrics: Location Accuracy (LA), Type Accuracy (TA), Propagation-path validity (PA), Hypothesis Accuracy (HA, requires both location AND type correct), all as top-k (A@1, A@3) and Avg@3. Random-guessing baselines computed per dataset.

- Best HA@3 on easier Dataset A (MicroSS): Qwen 3 (32B), Straight-Shot = **0.36** (random baseline HA@3 = 0.06).
- Best HA@3 on harder Dataset B (Online Boutique): Qwen 3 (32B), ReAct = **0.088**; Qwen 3 (4B), ReAct = 0.083 (random baseline HA@3 = 0.004).
- No single model or workflow dominates across all sub-tasks (Observation 1): Qwen 3 (32B) best overall HA; Llama 3.3 best PA (path grounding); Qwen 3 (4B) near-matches Qwen 3 (32B) on Straight-Shot/ReAct (within 1.2±3.5 points).
- **Agentic workflows show diminishing-to-negative returns on LA/TA/HA** (Observation 2): Straight-Shot often equals or beats ReAct and Plan-and-Execute for these metrics, especially for smaller models. Example: Llama 3.2 LA@3 falls **0.31 (Straight-Shot) → 0.23 (ReAct) → 0.05 (Plan-and-Execute)**. Below-random-guessing performance becomes more common as workflow complexity rises (TA below baseline for many model/workflow pairs; LA/HA below baseline mainly for smaller models under agentic workflows).
- **Agentic workflows do improve propagation-path validity (PA) for capable models** (Observation 3): e.g. Llama 3.3 on Dataset A: PA@3 = 0.61 (Straight-Shot) → 0.73 (ReAct) → 0.78 (Plan-and-Execute). This benefit is conditional on the model being able to reliably plan/execute tool use.
- **Execution/tool-use breakdown severely limits small models under Plan-and-Execute** (Observation 4): Qwen 3 (4B) and Llama 3.2 hit graph-recursion-limit/replanning failure rates of **87.3% and 77.3% respectively** under Plan-and-Execute. Qwen 3 (4B) explored the KG via tools in only **23.4% of ReAct samples (19.8% of non-error samples)** and **49.5% of Plan-and-Execute samples (67.8% of non-error samples)**, vs ~94.5% average tool coverage for other models — omitted tool calls force guessing/hallucination.

**RQ2 — modality sensitivity** (holdout ablation, Wilcoxon signed-rank test for significance, p<0.05):
- Withholding **metrics** most degrades localisation (ΔLA typically −0.07 to −0.15; Qwen 3 (4B) ΔLA = **−0.155**).
- Withholding **logs** most degrades fault-type attribution (ΔTA down to **−0.13**, e.g. Qwen 3 (4B) Plan-and-Execute ΔTA = −0.128).
- Withholding **traces** typically *improves* accuracy (ΔPA up to **+0.28**, ΔHA up to **+0.06**) — traces were noisy/voluminous and models over-focused on call relationships from them, drowning out other signal; removing them produced more focused, better-grounded reasoning. This is a genuine negative/counter-intuitive finding recorded with full diligence.

**RQ3 — reasoning-failure prevalence patterns** (qualitative, from Observations 8–11; no exact per-category percentages published in text):
- Procedural RFs (RF-09–12) show the widest spread across models/workflows — model-specific instability under agentic control. Llama 3.2 and Qwen 3 variants commit procedural errors far more under agentic workflows; direction of change from ReAct→Plan-and-Execute is itself model-dependent (rises for Llama 3.2, falls for Qwen 3). RF-16 (arithmetic errors) rare across all conditions.
- RCA-specific RFs (RF-02,03,06,07) show comparatively little variation across models/workflows — interpreted by authors as reflecting a shared deficit in domain-specific diagnostic reasoning rather than workflow sensitivity.
- General per-hypothesis RFs (RF-01,04,05,08) have the **highest average prevalence of all RF groups** — hypothesis claims are frequently under-justified or logically inconsistent regardless of model/workflow.
- Cross-cutting RFs (RF-13,14,15) vary by model family: Llama 3.2 and Command R+ show consistently higher rates (especially in longer multi-step traces); Qwen models show lower rates under both agentic workflows.

**RQ4 — association between RFs and correctness** (risk difference RD = p₁−p₀, relative risk RR = p₁/p₀; p₁ = P(correct | RF present)):
- Strongest negative predictors of correctness (location/type/hypothesis): **anchoring bias (RF-13), repetition/stalling (RF-12), arbitrary evidence selection (RF-07), failure to update belief (RF-09)** — at least a **15-point drop in correctness (RD < −0.15)** and correct predictions **≥45% less likely (RR < 0.55)** when present.
- For path existence specifically, the strongest negative associations come from **simulation/role confusion (RF-10), excessive speculation (RF-11), repetition (RF-12)**: RD < −0.29, RR < 0.32.
- Agentic workflows show smaller absolute RDs than Straight-Shot (per-RF marginal harm is diluted), **but agentic traces contain more RFs on average**: ReAct 5.1, Plan-and-Execute 4.8, Straight-Shot 4.6 RFs per trace. Authors interpret this as evidence that RF co-occurrence/compounding — not larger per-RF effect — plausibly explains why agentic workflows fail to reliably beat the non-agentic baseline on accuracy (RQ1).
- RF-10/RF-11 flip sign by workflow: positively associated with correctness under ReAct (RR > 1.49) but negatively under Plan-and-Execute (RR < 0.22); this co-occurs with execution errors in 68.6%/80.6% of Plan-and-Execute samples vs only 7.4%/0.5% of ReAct samples for RF-10/RF-11 respectively — i.e., the failure mode's meaning depends on whether it co-occurs with an outright execution crash.
- A small subset (RF-03, RF-08) shows a counter-intuitive *positive* RD, which the authors attribute to an aggregation artefact of their per-sample scoring scheme (correctness marked if *any* of 3 forced hypotheses is right) — explicitly flagged by the authors as an artefact, not a real protective effect. Recorded here as a methodological caveat, not a positive finding.

## Nulls / negatives / absences (recorded with equal diligence)

1. **No human-expert or human-developer RCA baseline anywhere in the study** — cannot support any AI-vs-human comparison for T8. This is a genuine scope gap, not a finding to fill in by inference.
2. **No frontier/proprietary agentic coding or AIOps tool was evaluated** (no GPT-4/5-class agent, no Claude Code/Cursor/Devin-class product). All 6 models are open-source and mid-tier by 2026 standards; results represent a capability floor, not the frontier.
3. **Traces (as a telemetry modality) were net-harmful to accuracy** in this setup — a negative result for a commonly-assumed-valuable data source, recorded above under RQ2.
4. **Agentic workflows (ReAct, Plan-and-Execute) do not reliably beat a simple single-pass baseline on root-cause/type/hypothesis accuracy**, and can fall below random-guessing for weaker models — a negative result for the general "agentic = better" narrative, though propagation-path grounding does improve for capable models.
5. **RF-16 (arithmetic/aggregation error) was rare across all conditions** — the one failure category the authors found not to be a meaningful problem for these models in this task.

## Grading

- **Tier:** Methodologically rigorous controlled benchmark experiment (large N = 48,000 scenarios, statistical significance testing via Wilcoxon signed-rank, inter-rater reliability κ=0.92, ablations, replication package released) — meets Tier A's rigour bar *as a capability/mechanism study*. However, per PROTOCOL §3, Tier A is defined around "RCTs, controlled field experiments, large-N telemetry with counterfactuals" for AI's effect on *outcomes* (implicitly human/task outcomes) — this source has no human subjects and no field setting at all. **Grade: Tier A for methodological rigour, but scope-restricted — it answers "where do LLMs fail at RCA reasoning in a controlled lab setting," not "does AI help incident response in the field."** Do not let its Tier-A rigour be read as Tier-A evidence for RQ1/RQ5-style claims.
- **Era:** Mixed by condition, tagged per protocol's modality rule. **ReAct and Plan-and-Execute = E3** (multi-step, tool-using, plan/execute/observe loops matching the protocol's E3 definition) — but using non-frontier open-source models, so treat as a lower bound within E3, not representative of frontier agentic coding tools. **Straight-Shot = closest to a single-pass "chat" condition**; it is fully automated (no human-in-the-loop copy-paste), so it doesn't cleanly match E2's definition either — tag it as a non-agentic LLM-reasoning baseline, era-ambiguous, admissible only as a methodology/contrast point, not as an E1/E2 field finding.
- **Independence:** Independent academic study, GM-of-Canada-funded (industrial sponsor, not an AI/RCA tool vendor) — COI risk low but disclosed per rule 7. No LLM vendor funding. GPT-5 used only as an arms-length judge (not one of the systems under evaluation), explicitly to avoid self-enhancement bias.
- **Population:** Not human developers — 6 open-source LLMs, 3 workflows, 2 synthetic microservice benchmark datasets (152 + 148 fault scenarios before scenario-multiplication to 48,000 runs via repeated sampling/ablation conditions).
- **Codebase context:** Small academic demo microservice apps (MicroSS: 24 entities, 5 fault categories; Online Boutique/Market: 60 entities, 15 fault categories), synthetic fault injection, single-fault-only scenarios — far below real production cloud scale/complexity.
- **Can support:** RQ3 (failure map / task-characteristics-predict-failure) as mechanism evidence for T8 — specifically that agentic RCA reasoning is fragile, that failure modes cluster into procedural/domain/general-logic categories, that certain failure types (anchoring, stalling, arbitrary evidence selection, failure to update belief) are the strongest correctness killers, and that "more tool-use loop" is not a reliable fix by itself. Can support RQ6 as a caution against trusting agentic-tool output without verification/evidence-sufficiency checks. Can support the cost/latency mechanism point under RQ4 (agentic overhead in compute time) at low external-validity weight.
- **Cannot support:** Any claim about human developer/SRE debugging speed, accuracy, or satisfaction (RQ1, RQ2 self-report/measured gap, RQ5 translation to production velocity/stability); any claim benchmarked against frontier proprietary agentic tools; any claim about real incident MTTR or on-call outcomes; any dollar-cost or licence-spend claim (RQ4's core ask).
