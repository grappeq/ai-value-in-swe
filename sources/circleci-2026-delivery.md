## What this document is
A vendor-published annual telemetry report from a CI/CD platform seller. It analyses 28,738,317 CircleCI workflows and argues that AI-driven code generation is raising CI activity, that most teams are not converting this into main-branch (shipped) throughput, and that validation/integration is the bottleneck. It ends with a pitch for CircleCI "autonomous validation". Bowley's post is an independent reading of the report's published numbers; the Thoughtworks piece is an opinion piece that restates the figures.

## Full notes

### The report
**Dataset (Methodology, p.25-26).**
- 28,738,317 workflows, "within the first 28 days of September 2025" (1-28 Sept 2025). Filters: projects with at least 2 contributors (all time); workflows that ran at least 5 times in the analysis period. Number of organisations or projects: not stated in the text I read ("thousands of teams" in the executive summary).
- Default-branch analysis uses the project's current default branch, so older data for projects that changed default branch may be missed. Industry data from Clearbit, not available for all organisations.
- The prior-year comparison window is not stated in the methodology; I infer September 2024. The companion blog repeats the September 2025 wording. Report credits include a Data Explorer to see the same dataset.
- Definitions: throughput = "the number of daily workflows run" (CI runs, not deployments). Success rate = workflow success rate on the main branch. MTTR = time to "get back to green" after a failed build (median, minutes). Main branch = the project's default branch; feature branches = non-default. "Median team", "top 10%" (p90), "top 5%" (p95) etc. are percentile bands of the distribution of daily throughput; chart titles read "median teams", "p90 teams", "p95 throughput". The report does not say whether bands are fixed cohorts tracked across years or percentiles re-formed each year, which matters for the YoY claims.

**Headline numbers.**
- Average throughput (daily workflows, all projects): +59% YoY.
- Throughput by band (daily runs; YoY): bottom 25% about 1, no measurable change; median 1.7, +4%; top 25% 3.3, +25%; top 10% 7.4, +47%; top 5% 6.8 to 13.4, +97% ("nearly doubled").
- Top 30 teams: all outperformed the prior-year equivalents; the leader ran ~10x 2024's leader (about 136,000 workflows/day; 135,888 in the chart); ranks 5-15 about 3x; ranks 20-30 about 2.5x. Top 10 teams each run over 10,000 workflows/day; the top 10 includes AI-powered software, utilities, consumer electronics, IT security, AI robotics and FinTech.
- By branch, median team: all branches +4.3%, feature +15.2%, main -6.8% (text: "declined 7%"). p90: all +47.2%, feature +49.3%, main +1.0%. p95: all +96.8%, feature +85.1%, main +26.2%. Report: "Fewer than 1 in 20 teams are currently able to absorb AI-driven acceleration without slowing down their delivery pipelines."
- Main-branch success rate: 70.8%, "lowest level in over 5 years" (chart axis 2022-2025). "Our recommended benchmark of 90%": a CircleCI-asserted benchmark, no derivation or external source given. The report concedes that CI failures are not always negative.
- MTTR: typical team 72 minutes, +13% YoY (chart 2023-2025, all vs feature branches); feature-branch MTTR +25% (64 to nearly 80 min). Main-branch median MTTR improved slightly to 59.2 min, below the 60-minute benchmark, "after rising sharply last year". Rising software-intensive-industry MTTR (computer software, financial services, capital markets, consumer goods): e.g. many were under an hour in 2024 and "now take 90 minutes or more".
- Duration: median workflow 2.2 min (2.7 in 2024, 18% faster); mean 9.9 min (11 in 2024, 10% faster).
- Splits: company size is U-shaped. Main-branch MTTR: 2-5 employees 51.7 min, 21-50 employees 174.4 min, 1000+ 46.5 min. By contributors: 50.2 / 151.4 / 33.2 min. Industry, country splits (median throughput; Italy, South Africa, Argentina, Czech Republic above major hubs). Language splits: none found. Org-size effects are descriptive.
- Illustrative cost arithmetic (not measured): at 5 changes/day, 90% to 70% success is one breakage every two days versus 1.5 per day; at 60 min recovery per failure, about 250 h/year; at 500 changes/day, about 12 full-time engineers.

**AI attribution.** The dataset contains no AI-usage variable that the report describes (no tool identifier, no agent-authorship flag, no adoption measure per team). The framing is interpretation, in the report's words: "Teams are producing more code than ever before, driven largely by growing adoption of AI-assisted development and autonomous coding agents"; "Average throughput increased 59% year over year, reflecting a sharp rise in AI-driven code generation"; "AI-driven changes are breaking more often"; on MTTR, "Since generative AI began to gain traction at the end of 2022, we've been tracking an increase in the time required to recover from failures". Also "AI-driven acceleration is amplifying existing delivery strengths". The report never shows AI-adopter versus non-adopter comparisons. Several top-10 high-throughput teams are listed as AI-powered software or AI robotics, which may reflect agents running on CI rather than human productivity.

**Series back to 2019.** Not available. The report shows 2022-2025 (success rate), 2023-2025 (MTTR), and 2023-2025 (top-30 throughput), plus 2024 vs 2025 for duration. "Lowest in over 5 years" implies an earlier history that is not published in the PDF. So whether 2025 is a break or a trend cannot be determined from the report; the MTTR claim of a rise since end-2022 is the only multi-year trend asserted, and the chart axes are 2023-2025. A later CircleCI Q2 2026 Pulse report exists (seen in search; not read).

**COI.** CircleCI sells CI/CD; more pipelines, more compute and more failures to fix all favour its product, and the final section sells "autonomous validation". The report also invites a Data Explorer comparison for lead generation. The 90% and 60-minute benchmarks are the vendor's own. The companion blog notes Thoughtworks as a sponsor of the 2026 edition (reported by the fetch tool; not independently confirmed).

### Critique (sub-section): Rob Bowley, 2 Apr 2026 (Tier C)
- Throughput in the report is "the number of times a CI pipeline runs per day", "not production deployments, it is not features shipped".
- The headline blends feature and main branches. He calls feature-branch throughput "effectively meaningless" as feature branches are not end-to-end until merged; main-branch throughput is the only meaningful measure. On main: median team -7%, top 10% "essentially flat" (+1.0%), so for ~95% of teams AI is generating more work that is not shipping. He quotes the 70.8% main-branch success rate as meaning 30% of attempts to merge for production are failing.
- "1 in 20": the top 5% is the only group with meaningful main-branch growth (+26%). He argues the claim is "less solid than it appears": the group's average pipeline duration is 6 seconds, "hard to think of a single CI step that legitimately completes in 6 seconds" (he suggests a possible report error), and one team reportedly ran about 130,000 workflows/day, which would have an outsized effect on aggregates. He finds the integration-bottleneck reading credible.
- My check of his arithmetic against the report's own tables: his main-branch figures match the report (median -6.8%, p90 +1.0%, p95 +26.2%; all-branch median +4.3%, p90 +47.2%). The 130,000 figure is consistent with the report's ~136K top team (the report's own top-team chart label is 135,888). I could not find the 6-second duration in the extracted text (the metrics summary page was unreadable), so it is unverified. His fetched summary contains no explicit derivation of "1 in 20" and no recomputed mean. My own reading (labelled as inference): 1-in-20 follows from the p95 cutoff chosen, not from a measured prevalence; because bands are formed on throughput rank, the p95 group is selected on the outcome. The p90 band (+1.0% on main) and p95 band (+26.2%) are percentile values, so strictly they say the 95th-percentile team now runs 26% more main-branch workflows, not that 5% of teams changed. The report's own tables support his main-branch point; they do not support a clean "1 in 20 benefit" reading.
- Caveat: the 59% figure is mean-driven (median +4%); the report itself makes the concentration point.

### Thoughtworks perspective (Rickey Zachary, 16 Mar 2026) - secondary colour only
Restates the report numbers; argues for platform engineering and DORA metrics; quotes Martin Fowler ("most organizations applying AI to software delivery are still treating it as a coding tool"). No new data, no methodology review, and it admits it does not know why builds fail. It carries a commercial interest in the framing. No weight beyond restatement.

## Tier & era grading
- Report: Tier D (vendor telemetry, CI-vendor COI). It is large-N telemetry but has no counterfactual and no AI variable, so it does not reach Tier A/B. Bowley critique: Tier C (independent practitioner, no new data). Thoughtworks piece: Tier D (consultancy), not extracted for claims.
- Era: UNSPLIT / indeterminate. The data contain no AI-usage variable, so the modality cannot be assigned by what was studied; the report's narrative asserts AI-assisted development and autonomous agents (E2/E3 mixed). Do not tag the numbers E3 on the report's authority. Per PROTOCOL §2 the findings can support at most descriptive context and the bottleneck-displacement hypothesis (§5.6) as plausible, not as an E3 effect.

## Measured vs self-reported
All figures are measured vendor telemetry (CI workflow counts, success rates, recovery times). There is no self-report component. The AI attribution is neither measured nor self-reported: it is the vendor's interpretation. Illustrative costs (250 h, 12 engineers) are modelled, not measured.

## Can / cannot support - verdicts
- CAN support: a Tier D observation that CI activity on feature branches grew while median main-branch throughput fell (-6.8%) and main-branch success rate fell to 70.8% in September 2025; consistent with the §5.6 bottleneck-displacement hypothesis; a RQ6 anti-metric critique (pipeline runs as "throughput"); a hype-baseline for the exec narrative.
- CANNOT support: any claim that AI caused these changes; any effect size for AI on delivery; "1 in 20 teams benefit from AI"; a productivity claim from the 59%; a break-versus-trend judgement (no 2019-2022 series published). No Tier D claim stands alone (PROTOCOL §8).

## Red flags / things the orchestrator should know
1. No AI variable in the data. Every AI statement is interpretation. Do not log any row as E3 effect.
2. The 59% is an average of a heavy-tailed distribution (median +4%; top team ~136K runs/day, ~10x last year's leader). Do not quote 59% without the median.
3. The 90% success-rate and 60-minute MTTR benchmarks are CircleCI's own and unsourced; the report concedes failed CI runs can be good.
4. The report's own main-branch MTTR improved (59.2 min) and workflow duration fell (median -18%), which complicates the "breaking more and taking longer to fix" headline; the 72 min / +13% figure is all-branch.
5. Single month (Sept 2025), unclear YoY comparison window and cohort definition (fixed teams versus percentile bands); possible selection effects on top-band groups; automated/agent traffic may inflate workflow counts.
6. The summary-metrics page and chart curves were unreadable; no organisation or project count found. Bowley's and Thoughtworks' texts were read via summarising fetches. A version-check on the 90 days since publication (Q2 2026 Pulse report, reportedly a 9x top-versus-median gap on main) was seen only in a search snippet and not read; consider as a possible supersession check.
7. The PDF was not readable by the standard extractor; a Tier D source, so not a PROTOCOL §3 blocker.
