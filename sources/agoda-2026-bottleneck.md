## Method & what Agoda actually measured vs asserted

**There is no described measurement methodology in this piece.** Stern's post is an argumentative essay applying Fred Brooks' "No Silver Bullet" logic to AI coding tools, not a report of an internal study, telemetry pull, or survey. The one sentence that gestures at an internal investigation — *"When we dug into why the expected gains weren't materializing, we were reminded of something the industry has collectively forgotten: coding is only a small part of the software development lifecycle"* — is immediately followed by an illustrative example, not a reported result, and no data, dashboard, or methodology is described anywhere in the piece. **This ambiguity is the single most important interpretive call in this record and is flagged rather than resolved:** it is not possible to tell from the text whether "we dug into" refers to a real internal Agoda review that produced an unstated finding later dressed up in a generic illustration, or whether the phrase is purely rhetorical scaffolding for the argument that follows. Treat as **asserted/narrative, not measured**, per the ambiguity.

**Numeric content in the post, exhaustively, with attribution (verbatim-quote-checked):**

1. *"Speed up only that part, and you get 10× faster code generation, but only 10% faster shipping. The bottleneck simply moves elsewhere."* — **Not attributed to any measurement.** Confirmed by paragraph-context check: this sentence functions as a generic illustrative example of the Amdahl's-law-style principle Stern is about to invoke via Brooks, not a reported Agoda statistic. **No other numbers appear in the post.** No Agoda team size, no AI tool adoption rate, no before/after velocity metric, no review-time figure, no defect or incident data, no cost/token-spend figure. Every substantive claim beyond the two numbers above is narrative/asserted:

- Timeline for organisational adaptation "one to two years" — asserted, no basis given.

## The white box / black box / grey box framing (verbatim)

Quoted directly from the post:

> "White box is the traditional model: humans implement and review line-by-line... [this] does not scale when agents can produce thousands of lines hourly."
> "Black box, often called 'vibe coding', sits at the other extreme: trust the AI, ship what it generates, minimal verification... fast but brittle for production systems serving large user bases."
> "Grey box names the middle ground: we accept that humans won't read everything an agent writes, but we also refuse blind trust" — keeping humans accountable at two points: **writing specifications precise enough for the agent to execute correctly**, and **verifying results against evidence rather than inspecting the implementation line by line**.

Stern is explicit that accountability does not transfer to the AI: "the engineer guiding the agent and the reviewer approving the merge request remain fully accountable for what ships." This is a conceptual taxonomy, offered with no adoption data (no figure for what share of Agoda's own PRs are handled white/black/grey-box) and no outcome data comparing the three modes.

## What specifically is claimed not to have sped up

Per the post: **specification/requirements work and verification/review**, not deployment or planning-as-project-management. Exact framing: "the real bottleneck has shifted upstream to specification and verification, two areas where human judgment matters most." Deployment, CI/CD, and operations are not discussed in this piece at all — the bottleneck claim is scoped narrowly to spec-writing and verification/review, not the full SDLC. This narrows the claim relative to how the task brief's framing ("review? planning? deployment?") might suggest all three are addressed — only two of those three are, and only asserted, not measured.

## What this can and cannot support

**Can support:** a genuine, independently-arrived-at, named-practitioner (non-vendor, engineering-leadership) articulation of the bottleneck-displacement hypothesis — coding-phase acceleration outpacing gains in specification and verification — expressed through a memorable and citable conceptual framework (white/black/grey box) that is useful for the exec brief's *narrative* register regardless of its evidentiary thinness. Useful as one qualitative data point in a triangulation set for the *existence and direction* of the pattern, satisfying the §3 independence test on provenance (different org, self-generated argument, no shared vendor sponsorship with Faros/DX/Zendesk/He et al.).

**Cannot support:** any effect size, any Agoda-specific measured claim, any claim that Agoda "measured" a gap between individual and project-level velocity (this is asserted, not instrumented, in the primary text), and no claim that deployment or planning specifically failed to speed up (only specification and verification are addressed). Cannot be one of the "≥3 independent sources" needed to jointly carry a *numeric* exec-brief claim under §3, because it supplies no number of its own. Cannot be used interchangeably with the Faros 21%/98%/91% figures, which are Faros's numbers, reported by InfoQ, not Agoda's.
