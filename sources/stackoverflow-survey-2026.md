# Source: stackoverflow-survey-2026

## Identity
- **Title:** Stack Overflow Annual Developer Survey 2026 (16th edition), AI chapter plus hub, technology and methodology pages
- **Publisher:** Stack Overflow (Prosus-owned). Survey instrument in the public StackExchange/Survey repository; the full dataset is promised "later" on GitHub (not yet seen).
- **Dates:** fielded 23 June to 5 August 2026 (methodology page; the blog says "seven weeks"). Published 6 October 2026.
- **URLs read:** https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2026/ ; https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2026/ai ; https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2026/ai/data (and its `.md` rendering, which carried the full tables); https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2026/technology ; https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2026/methodology ; https://stackoverflow.blog/2026/10/06/the-results-of-the-2026-developer-survey-are-here/ ; press release https://stackoverflow.co/company/press/archive/stack-overflow-annual-developer-survey-ai-trust-conditional/ ; podcast page https://stackoverflow.blog/2026/10/06/tales-from-the-2026-developer-survey-results/ (no data); LeadDev piece (turned out to be 2025 survey coverage, used only for the prior-year trust figure).
- **Format:** web-published survey tables with per-question n; blog; press release. Raw data not yet released, so no re-analysis was possible.
- **Identity/date verification:** all four assigned pages resolved and agree on edition, N (30,903 valid responses, 169 countries, 467 technologies, 103 questions) and publication date. Web search returned independent trade coverage (IT Brief, SecurityBrief) repeating the 6.6% trust and 78.7% attribution figures.
- **Method of reading:** the fetch tool returns model-processed page text, not raw HTML. Table values quoted below come from the `data.md` rendering and were cross-checked against the other pages. Items marked "(blog)" or "(hub)" were seen only in prose and have no verified base n.
- **Recency check:** not required (under 12 months old). Prior-year comparison point: the 2025 survey reported 33% trusting AI output accuracy (43% in 2024) and 60% favourable (72% in 2024), per LeadDev's 2025 coverage; the 2026 trust question is worded differently and is not comparable.

## What this document is
The annual Stack Overflow opt-in web survey of developers, with a large AI chapter (usage, frequency, trust, validation, task use, usefulness, satisfaction, organisational policy, tool rankings). It contains no productivity, time-saving, throughput or quality measurement. It is an adoption, sentiment and self-reported-practice instrument. Recruitment was via stackoverflow.com, the blog and an opted-in email list; no sampling frame or weighting is described.

## Full notes

### Sample and method
- Extract has 39,266 rows, 30,903 marked valid (consent, employment answered, not bot-flagged, with a recovery rule for bot-flagged responses that answered ten core questions). Bot flagging uses Qualtrics signals (reCAPTCHA, straightlining, duration and so on).
- Base n varies by question, from about 1,400 (agent memory tools) to 17,464 (AI tool usage). Typical AI-chapter n is 12,000 to 14,500.
- Methodology page states the sample is self-selected from Stack Overflow channels; gender is 86% "Man", unadjusted. No weighting.
- No year-over-year comparison appears in the AI data tables. The hub gives some year-on-year items outside AI (freelance share). One hub line reading "87%+ have some trust in AI output, up from 31% last year" is untraceable and contradicts the AI tables (see red flags); I do not rely on it.

### General AI usage (not agent-specific)
- **AISelect** (multi-select, n = 17,464): "Do you currently use AI tools or AI agents in your role at work?" AI coding assistants or coding agents 65.9% (11,509); general-purpose AI chat tools 62.5% (10,920); AI agents or automated workflows 26.2% (4,582); internal AI tools built by my company 17.8% (3,115); I don't use AI tools 17.2% (3,003). Hence about 83% use at least one.
- **AIFreq** (n = 14,478): "How often do you use each of these AI tools or AI agents in your role at work?" Daily: coding assistants/agents 73.0% (weekly 20.1%, monthly/infrequent 6.2%); general chat 65.6%; agents/automated workflows 63.1%; internal tools 47.7%. Overall 77% daily. The 73% is a share of coding-assistant users, not of all respondents.
- **AIDaily** (n = 10,926; asked of daily users): "How much of your workday do you use AI tools to help you work?" 4+ hours 30.9%; 2 to 4 hours 24.1%; 1 to 2 hours 25.3%; under 1 hour every day 12.2%; under 1 hour, not every day 7.0%; none 0.5%. Combined 2+ hours = 55.0%; 1+ hour = 80.3%. The hub's "80% use AI at least an hour per day" is therefore 80% of daily users, not of all respondents.
- **AISentiment** (n = 14,428): "How would you describe your attitude toward using AI tools or agents as part of your work?" Very favourable 31.6%; somewhat favourable 30.4% (62% favourable); neutral 15.3%; somewhat unfavourable 14.6%; very unfavourable 6.9%; not sure 1.2%. Favourable 53% for 1 to 5 years' experience versus 69% for 16+ years; 71% among daily users (blog). The bases for the experience splits are not stated.
- **AIAvoid** (n = 13,857): reasons to avoid AI at work or school. Do not avoid 41.3%; losing job skills / training a replacement 17.1%; moral or ethical 15.2%; privacy or security 13.0%; environmental 8.2%; difficulty or lack of training 2.6%; other 2.7%. About 58.7% cite some reason (page headline: "6 out of 10").
- **AIToolCount** (n = 11,919): tools used weekly: 1 = 19.7%; 2 = 34.4%; 3 to 5 = 40.5%; 6 to 10 = 3.8%; 11+ = 1.0%.
- **AIRetain** (n = 12,825): who should control AI memory: just me 53.6%; organisation centrally 14.7%; do not want AI to retain memory 14.2%; my team 11.1%.

### Agent items (kept separate)
- **AICodeAgent** (n = 12,255; used in past year; question wording not captured verbatim): Claude Code 65.5% (8,030); GitHub Copilot 58.7% (7,196); OpenAI Codex 29.5%; Cursor 25.5%; Google Antigravity 16.0%; Gemini Code Assist 14.6%; Open Code 13.6%; JetBrains AI 12.8%; Windsurf 6.5%; Warp 5.2%; Cline 4.5%; Pi 4.4%; Amazon Q Developer 3.9%; Continue 3.8%; Mistral Vibe 3.2%; Kilo Code 3.2%; Devin 2.9%; Google Jules 2.5%; Replit Agent 2.3%; Tabnine 2.1%; Aider 2.1%; long tail below 2%. Multi-select, so shares overlap; this is the share of respondents answering this question, not of all respondents. The list mixes agentic tools (Claude Code, Codex, Devin) with autocomplete-heavy products (Copilot, Tabnine, Supermaven); the survey does not say which mode of Copilot or Cursor was used.
- **Agents or automated workflows at work** (AISelect): 26.2% (n = 17,464); 63.1% of those use them daily (n within AIFreq).
- Other agent-stack rankings: orchestration (n = 4,400) LangChain 30.2%, OpenAI Agents SDK 23.8%; observability/evals (n = 2,277) Sentry 26.0%, LangSmith 17.3%; agent memory (n = 1,392) Mem0 20.0%; no-code builders (n = 2,219) Lovable 38.3%; workflow automation (n = 4,173) Make 38.9%, n8n 32.7%. These are agent-building, not coding-agent, items.
- LLM provider use (technology page, n = 11,895): Anthropic 74.6%; OpenAI 74.3%; Google 54.5%; Microsoft 26.4%. Want to use next year: Claude 49% versus OpenAI 32%. The technology page says it does not break out specific coding agents (separate from AICodeAgent above).
- 76.5% (12,423) used an IDE "to write code or work with agent-assisted developer tools" (technology page); this item bundles IDEs with agent tools and cannot measure agent adoption.
- Context for agents (hub): 85% say project goals or requirements are the most important context for AI agents; 79% often discover key context only after starting or finishing a task; 63% cite incomplete information as a barrier. Bases not captured.

### Trust items
- **AITrust** (n = 14,304): "How much do you trust the output from AI tools or AI agents as part of your workflow?" Trust it when I can easily verify the output 48.0% (6,872); trust for many tasks, not important work decisions 16.3%; trust for low-risk work tasks only 16.3%; do not trust it for most work tasks 12.3%; trust for many tasks including important decisions 6.6% (949); not sure/don't use 0.5%.
- Early-career (data page experience split): 4% of early-career users trust AI for important decisions versus 6.6% overall. Band definition and base not stated.
- **Attribution** (press release): 78.7% say source attribution is important or very important when evaluating AI-generated code or technical answers; the hub says 93% "say citing sources is important for verifying AI answers", and the blog says 93% say attribution is needed to trust output. These are different thresholds or wordings of one question or of different questions; not reconcilable from what was read.
- **AIComplex** (n = 14,341): "How well do the AI tools or agents you use handle complex tasks?" Very well 16.0%; good but not great 38.8%; neither 14.4%; bad 16.0%; very poor 8.0%; don't use for complex tasks/don't know 6.9%. Bad or very poor = 24.0%. The page states 45% of infrequent users rate performance bad or very poor; no daily-user figure given.
- **AIWorkNext** (n = 13,162, multi-select): "What do you usually do before using an AI-generated answer for a development task?" Run it locally 76.5%; compare against codebase context 63.9%; inspect tests/security implications 53.0%; read official docs 38.0%; search Stack Overflow 12.2%; use as-is 9.6%; ask a coworker 6.9%; do not use AI tools 1.7%.
- No item on "almost right but not quite" output, time spent debugging AI-generated code, or AI-attributed incidents was found in the pages read. One technology-page item says 23% (chart: 23.5%, 3,205) "lost valuable time to AI-related security or privacy issues"; its base is not stated and the wording is only paraphrased, so treat as unverified.
- Prior-year reference (2025, via LeadDev, not re-verified at source): 33% trusted accuracy (43% in 2024); 69% of agent users at work agreed productivity was improving. Wording differs from 2026.

### Task use, usefulness, satisfaction (all self-report)
- **AIWork** (n = 12,547): current use / expect more: generating code familiar area 69.3 / 31.2; debugging, troubleshooting, refactoring 63.8 / 36.1; straightforward technical questions 59.4; writing or improving tests 58.1 / 35.0; generating code unfamiliar area 56.0; writing documentation 51.1; complex/ambiguous technical questions 50.0; searching internal knowledge 49.7; understanding a new codebase 49.7; architecture/design exploration 47.4; analysing data/logs/metrics 41.9; reviewing code, PRs, design decisions 40.5 (22.6 will not use); finding/fixing security issues 34.6; product/UX design 30.3; creating/configuring dev/test environments 28.1; writing tickets/requirements/plans 27.8; **deploying/operating/troubleshooting production 19.9 (36.0 do not use and do not plan to; 22.0 not part of workflow)**; emails 23.5; multimedia 17.5.
- **AIImpact** (n = 12,734; useful plus very useful, page figures/computed): implementation 74.6% (page says 75%); debugging/refactoring 72.6%; documentation 65.7%; review 64.5%; architecture exploration 59.1%; design 49.2%; communication 35.0%. Impractical/not useful: implementation 9.0%, communication 22.6%.
- **AISat** (n = 12,125; improved/decreased/no change, computed): debugging/refactoring 66.1/10.5/23.4; documentation 60.6/9.8/29.6; implementation 60.1/19.9/19.9; review 55.3/11.9/32.8; architecture 54.8/9.3/35.8; design 47.6/13.1/39.4; communication 32.7/17.9/49.5. Implementation is the most polarised task (about one in five report decreased satisfaction). The exact question wording was not captured (it asks about change in satisfaction with the task since using AI).
- Build context (technology page, n = 13,338): the page says AI adoption drops as system complexity and operational risk rise. The underlying cross-tab was not captured, so this is a prose statement only.

### Cost, usage limits, organisational policy
- **AIToolSwitch** (n = 13,144): 66.3% (computed 65.7% by the page) switched tools in the past year. Reasons: better output quality 25.1%; organisation required or recommended another 10.6%; better workflow/IDE integration 7.4%; lower cost 6.4%; better agent/automation support 3.7%; previous tool too expensive, limited or unavailable 3.3%.
- **AIUse** (n = 13,167): organisational use is mostly optional 29.7%; approved tools and guidance 24.0%; some teams encourage use 15.9%; expects some employees to use AI 11.5%; measures/evaluates AI use in performance 10.2%; organisation does not use AI 6.1%.
- **AITry** (n = 12,637): a few approved tools 32.8%; mostly one approved tool 18.6%; teams choose their own 17.3%; trying multiple 12.5%; building internal tools or models 4.3%.
- **Cost governance (blog only; base and wording not captured):** organisations use usage limits 38%, review of spend data 28%, different models for different tasks 27%, limiting who can use AI 25%; 21% say their organisation is spending more on AI; "75% prioritise quality over cost (38%)" when choosing models and prompts (ambiguous as reported). I could not find these items in the data tables on the pages I read. They are plausible but unverified at the table level.
- No per-seat price, token spend, or per-task cost data exists in the survey.
- Hub: 19% build tools to replace paid vendors; technology page: 54.4% (1,349) of those replacing paid vendors used AI to do so.

### Productivity and quality
- No productivity, speed-up, throughput or quality measure was found. "Useful" and "satisfaction change" are the nearest items and are about perceived usefulness, not output.
- 64% visit Stack Overflow less often for simple questions because of AI; 70% ask an AI agent when finding answers (hub; base not captured). Context note: Stack Overflow is a party with an interest in this finding.

## Tier & era grading
- **Tier:** C for adoption, practice and sentiment facts (large-N, practitioner-sourced, but non-random and self-selected); no Tier B claim because there is no sampling frame, weighting or longitudinal design for these items. Tier D flag applies to commercial interest (below). Net: Tier C, directional only, and not usable for effect sizes of any kind.
- **Era:** mixed. Coding-agent items (AICodeAgent, agents/automated workflows, agent-builder tools) are E3-tagged by modality. "AI coding assistants or coding agents" is a bundled category spanning E1 to E3 and cannot be split. General chat tools are E2. Trust, sentiment, usefulness and satisfaction items ask about "AI tools or agents" generally, so they are mixed E2/E3 and cannot be attributed to agentic work. Treat these as era-independent self-report baselines or sentiment trend evidence, not E3 effect evidence.
- **Independence/COI:** Stack Overflow sells AI-related products and data licensing (Overflow API / enterprise knowledge products) and runs a Q&A community whose traffic AI has reduced; it has an interest in framing both "trust is conditional" and "source attribution matters" (the press release headline) and a new "knowledge, trust and context" section. The blog and press release emphasise attribution (78.7% or 93%), which supports its own product positioning. Respondents are drawn from its channels, which over-represent Stack Overflow-engaged developers and likely under-represent non-users and those who left the platform.
- **Population:** developers answering an opt-in survey; seniority mostly unreported for AI items; codebase context not captured.

## Measured vs self-reported
Everything here is self-reported. Nothing is measured: no telemetry, no timed tasks, no verified output quality. Items that might read as measured but are not: "hours of the workday using AI" (self-estimated time), the validation-practice shares (stated habits, not observed behaviour), satisfaction "improved" (perceived), and the 23.5% "lost valuable time" figure (self-recalled). No perceived-versus-measured delta can be computed from this source. Given the METR perception-reality gap (PROTOCOL §5.1), these items cannot serve as impact evidence.

## Can / cannot support - verdicts
- **Can support:** adoption and frequency levels among Stack Overflow-reachable developers in mid-2026; the coding-agent tool ranking (Claude Code first, Copilot second) as a popularity snapshot; the pattern that trust is conditional on verification (48% when easily verified; 6.6% for important decisions); that verification practice is stated as near-universal (about 90% do not use output as-is); that usage thins towards production operations.
- **Can support (weakly):** a perception that AI is useful and satisfaction-improving for debugging, implementation and documentation, and less so for design and communication; a hint of seniority divergence in sentiment (53% versus 69%).
- **Cannot support:** any effect size on speed, throughput, quality, or cost; any claim about brownfield versus greenfield (no codebase context); any claim about review bottlenecks or defect rates; population-level adoption (self-selected); any year-over-year trend (wording changed; no 2026 comparison in the tables); any E3-specific effect (items bundle modalities).

## Red flags / things the orchestrator should know
1. **6.6% versus "7%".** The data table gives 6.6% (949 of 14,304) for "trust for many tasks, including important decisions"; the page summary text rounds to 7%; the press release says "only 6.6% would trust AI with important decisions". Use 6.6%.
2. **Hub "87%+ have some trust in AI output, up from 31% last year".** I could not trace this in the AI tables. Adding the trust categories gives about 99% with some trust, but with 12.3% "do not trust for most tasks" and the 2025 trust-accuracy figure at 33%, the hub line looks like a garbled or differently defined statistic. Do not cite it. It also reads as contradicting the "conditional trust" headline.
3. **Debugging figure mix-up.** The press release says AI is useful for debugging or refactoring at "66%", while the data page gives 72.6% usefulness (page text 73%) and 66.1% for satisfaction improvement. The 66% appears to be the satisfaction figure mislabelled as usefulness. The press release also gives implementation as 74% (page: 75%; computed 74.6%).
4. **Attribution 78.7% versus 93%.** Press release: 78.7% important or very important. Hub and blog: 93%. Different wording or base; unresolved.
5. **Percentages with different bases look alike.** "80% use AI at least an hour per day" is 80% of daily users (n = 10,926), not of respondents. "73% daily" is of coding assistant users. The "66% use coding agents" (blog: "of AI users") is actually 65.9% of all n = 17,464 respondents to the AI-usage question, which includes the 17.2% who do not use AI; the blog's "of AI users" phrasing is inaccurate.
6. **Claude Code 66% base.** The 65.5% is of 12,255 who answered the coding-agent question, not of all respondents and not of AI users; coincidence with the 65.9% usage figure invites confusion. Because this is a respondent-selected multi-select over a list that includes legacy autocomplete products, it is not a measure of agentic adoption in the sense of PROTOCOL §2.
7. **Blog-only cost items** (usage limits 38%, spend review 28%, and so on): not found in the data tables, with no base or wording. Treat as unverified until the raw data or table is located.
8. **Freelance figure inconsistent:** hub 3.9% to 10.5%; blog "4% to 10%" and elsewhere "stayed about the same"; press release 11% up from 4%. Not AI-relevant but indicates loose editing. It also suggests population composition shifted year to year (more sole proprietors), which weakens any year-over-year AI comparison.
9. **Respondent-count discrepancy:** blog mentions a chart with 16,664 respondents; AI items use n between about 1,400 and 17,464. Fine, but the headline N (30,903) is never the base for an AI item.
10. **Fetch-tool caveat:** pages were read through a model-summarising fetch tool; the `data.md` rendering gave full tables but questions beyond those quoted were paraphrased. Wording for AICodeAgent and AISat should be confirmed against the instrument in the StackExchange/Survey repository before quoting.
11. Trust-by-experience beyond the 4% early-career figure, and any per-band breakdown of agent use, were not available.
